# Blueprint-Vision Applied Practice

The PRACTITIONER-KNOWLEDGE layer for the blueprint-vision galaxy: the hard-won "tribal" gotchas a world-class drawing-reader / inspection engineer carries that pure theory does not teach. DISTINCT from the two siblings:
- `blueprint-vision-foundations.md` — the *theory* (GD&T families, FCF structure, MMC/LMC definitions, NIST digital thread, machine-vision/signal/optics/statistics grounding). This entry does not re-explain those; it documents **what goes wrong and how an expert avoids it**.
- `blueprint-vision-source-atlas.md` — the *link directory*. This entry asserts technique, not links.

Each item below = the gotcha + WHY it bites + the expert's avoidance, with the confirming source cited inline. Because this is a drawing-reading / inspection (non-cutting) domain, the knowledge is qualitative technique; specific tolerance/DPI numbers stay owner-gated unless they are a published source's structural fact.

## Common failure modes (GD&T misreads that scrap or false-pass parts)

- **Datum-reference order is load-bearing — reversing the datum letters silently changes the inspection setup.** The order of datums in a feature control frame "will dictate which features take precedence when locking down the datum reference frame for inspection": the **primary datum (A)** constrains three degrees of freedom, the **secondary (B)** two more, the **tertiary (C)** the last one. Why it bites: a parser (or a human) that treats `|pos|Ø0.x|A|B|C|` and `|pos|Ø0.x|B|A|C|` as equivalent has lost the setup geometry — "reversing this order would change which constraints apply when, potentially allowing non-functional parts to pass." Expert avoidance: the extractor must preserve datum order as an *ordered* field (never a set), and datums should be chosen "in the order that the part would assemble in real life," not by convenience. [confirmed via [GD&T Basics — Datum Reference Frame](https://www.gdandtbasics.com/datum-reference-frame/)]

- **Bonus tolerance exists ONLY when an MMC/LMC modifier is present — granting or denying it wrongly rejects good parts or passes bad ones.** "Only when the MMC or the LMC symbol is called out do you get a 'bonus' tolerance"; with no modifier the feature defaults to RFS and "no bonus tolerance exists." Why it bites: if an inspector ignores available bonus and measures an MMC feature as if RFS applied, "a functionally acceptable part can be wrongly rejected"; conversely "treating an RFS callout as if MMC were present creates false acceptance." Expert avoidance: the extractor must capture the modifier glyph as a *first-class* field and treat absence-of-modifier as a positive RFS signal — "an inspector who grants bonus without seeing the modifier is inventing tolerance; one who denies it despite seeing it is ignoring design intent." [confirmed via [GD&T Basics — Maximum Material Condition (MMC)](https://www.gdandtbasics.com/maximum-material-condition/)]

- **A position callout WITHOUT the diameter symbol means a square zone — and quietly throws away most of the tolerance.** When position is given as a coordinate distance (±X, ±Y) "the tolerance zone forms a square"; the Ø symbol makes it a round/cylindrical zone. Why it bites: the published source states omitting the diameter symbol "removed over 57% of your tolerance zone" because a circle inscribed in that square loses the corner area — and worse, "a hole location that passes the square tolerance zone might actually be unusable functionally." Expert avoidance: the FCF parser must detect the presence/absence of the leading Ø in the tolerance compartment and never normalize a square (coordinate) zone and a diametral zone to the same shape. [confirmed via [GD&T Basics — True Position](https://www.gdandtbasics.com/true-position/)]

- **A projected-tolerance-zone (circled P) hole can pass position at the surface and still jam the assembly.** The modifier means the tolerance zone is "assessed beyond the surface extents of the feature," projected outward to at least the thickness of the mating part. Why it bites: ignoring it is "the most common mistake" — a tapped hole can "remain within tolerance of position but have an interference on the mating part" because the *axis tilt* only matters above the surface where the stud/screw projects. Expert avoidance: the extractor must flag the circled-P modifier and propagate it to any downstream fit/assembly check; a hole's position result is incomplete without the projected-zone height when the modifier is present. [confirmed via [GD&T Basics — Projected Tolerance Zone](https://www.gdandtbasics.com/projected-tolerance-zone/)]

## Technique decisions (the judgment calls an expert makes)

- **Basic dimensions are theoretically exact and have NO tolerance — never parse them as toleranced.** A basic dimension (shown boxed) "describe[s] a feature's theoretically exact location, orientation, or size. Therefore, they have no size tolerance associated with them," and "are not inspected directly. However, they are measured indirectly by GD&T position callouts." Why it bites: an extractor that attaches a default title-block tolerance to a boxed basic dimension corrupts the geometric control — the boxed value is the *origin* the FCF tolerance is measured from, not a ±-able number. Expert avoidance: treat the box glyph as a hard type-discriminator (basic vs limit/plus-minus), and route basic values into the position/profile reference, not the size-limits field. [confirmed via [Machining Doctor — GD&T](https://www.machiningdoctor.com/gdt/)]

- **Worst-case vs RSS tolerance-stack is a deliberate choice, not a formula default — using the wrong one over-tightens (cost) or under-protects (scrap).** Worst-case sums tolerances arithmetically ("WC = Σ|tol_i|") and "ensures 100% of assemblies will fit" but is conservative and "carries a manufacturing cost penalty"; RSS combines them statistically ("RSS = √(Σtol_i²)"), yields a tighter band and lower cost, but "does *not* guarantee 100% conformance" (≈99.73% at 3σ). Why it bites: a tolerance analyzer that hard-codes one method will either demand needlessly tight features on a high-volume part or pass a safety-critical interface that should have used worst-case. Expert avoidance: worst-case "particularly appropriate for critical safety applications," RSS for "high-volume production" / "cost-sensitive applications" — the method is an input the analysis must surface, not assume. [confirmed via [FIRGELLI — Tolerance Stack-Up Calculator (Worst Case and RSS)](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/engineering-calculators/tolerance-stack-up-calculator-worst-case-and-rss)]

- **An inclined or oblique surface is foreshortened in the standard views — its true shape lives in an auxiliary view.** "A surface inclined to a plane of projection shows foreshortened," so "its true dimensions cannot be measured directly"; an auxiliary view (looking perpendicular to the slanting surface) is drawn "to show a slanting surface in its true shape." Why it bites: a view-association stage that pulls a dimension off a foreshortened face in a principal view reads a compressed (wrong) length. Expert avoidance: detect auxiliary views and bind angled-feature dimensions to them; never trust a length measured off a face the projection compresses. [confirmed via [French & Svensen, *Mechanical Drawing for High Schools* (1919), public-domain](https://archive.org/stream/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich_djvu.txt)]

## Drawing-reading gotchas (views, lines, sections)

- **Dotted/dashed lines are hidden features — a line-type misclassification changes a feature's semantic status.** "All surfaces must be represented whether they can actually be seen or not... surfaces which cannot be seen in the views... are represented by dotted lines." Why it bites: if the OCR/vectorizer collapses a dashed hidden line into a solid visible edge (or vice versa), the part's interior/exterior topology is silently wrong — a hidden bore becomes a visible slot. Expert avoidance: the line-type classifier (solid vs dashed/hidden vs center) is a first-class extraction stage that runs *before* geometry interpretation, not a cosmetic afterthought. [confirmed via [French & Svensen, *Mechanical Drawing for High Schools* (1919), public-domain](https://archive.org/stream/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich_djvu.txt)]

- **A sectional view shows interior detail and is marked by cross-hatching — hatching is not a real surface.** "A sectional view is a view obtained by supposing the piece to be cut apart and the front portion removed, thus exposing the interior," with "the cut surface... indicated by cross-hatching, or section lining, with uniformly spaced fine lines." Why it bites: a naive raster pipeline can read the regularly-spaced hatch lines as periodic texture/noise or as part edges, and a frequency-domain denoiser can even *erase* them. Expert avoidance: recognize section hatching as a distinct region label (cut material), exclude it from edge/feature detection, and use it as a cue that the view is a section. [confirmed via [French & Svensen, *Mechanical Drawing for High Schools* (1919), public-domain](https://archive.org/stream/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich/mechanicaldrawin00frenrich_djvu.txt)]

## Title-block & revision traps (the metadata that overrides the geometry)

- **The title-block / "Unless Otherwise Specified" note is the default tolerance for any un-toleranced dimension — and an explicit callout always overrides it, even when the explicit value is LOOSER.** "Any dimension without an explicit tolerance uses these values" (the UOS block); a dimension with its own callout "overrides the title-block default, even if the block tolerance is looser." Why it bites: an extractor that ignores the block leaves every plain dimension tolerance-less; one that always applies the block over-constrains a feature the designer *intentionally* relaxed (e.g. ±0.010 where the block is ±0.005). Expert avoidance: parse the UOS/general-tolerance note as the fallback layer, apply per-decimal-place defaults only to dimensions lacking an explicit ± / limit / GD&T control, and let any explicit callout win. [confirmed via [MakerStage — How to Read Engineering Drawings](https://www.makerstage.com/resources/how-to-read-engineering-drawings)]

- **"DO NOT SCALE DRAWING" means the printed geometry is NOT authoritative — only the stated numbers are.** Printed drawings "can shift due to paper shrinkage, PDF zoom, or printing error... physical measurements from paper are forbidden. Only use the stated numbers." Why it bites: a blueprint-vision pipeline that *measures a dimension from pixels* (scale-from-image) when the printed dimension text exists is committing exactly the forbidden operation — and the printout's DPI/zoom makes the pixel measure unreliable. Expert avoidance: the parsed dimension *text* is authoritative; a pixel-measured estimate may corroborate or flag a low-confidence OCR read, but must never silently override the printed value. [confirmed via [MakerStage — How to Read Engineering Drawings](https://www.makerstage.com/resources/how-to-read-engineering-drawings)]

- **Always read to the LATEST revision — an outdated rev is one of the easiest ways to make the wrong part.** The revision block lists "revision letter, description of change, date, and approver," and the rule is "Always manufacture to the latest revision" — "two revisions can change critical features, tolerances, or entire sections." Why it bites: an extraction pipeline that does not surface the revision letter lets a downstream consumer quote/cut from a stale drawing. Expert avoidance: capture the revision letter + date as required metadata and treat a missing/ambiguous revision as a hard low-confidence flag, not a silent default. [confirmed via [MakerStage — How to Read Engineering Drawings](https://www.makerstage.com/resources/how-to-read-engineering-drawings)]

## OCR / machine-vision failure modes (noisy or hand-marked scans)

- **Under-resolution silently destroys glyph recognition — feed the OCR enough pixels per character.** Tesseract "performs optimally at a DPI of at least 300 dpi," and small images should be rescaled up before recognition. Why it bites: thin dimension lines and small GD&T glyphs under-sample at low DPI (a sampling-theory aliasing failure), so the recognizer never sees a clean character. Expert avoidance: enforce a minimum capture resolution / up-scale tiny crops before recognition; resolution lost at capture cannot be recovered by any downstream model. [confirmed via [Tesseract OCR — Improve Quality](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html)]

- **Global binarization fails on unevenly-lit or hand-photographed drawings — use adaptive thresholding.** Tesseract's internal Otsu binarization "can be suboptimal, particularly if the page background is of uneven darkness"; OpenCV's docs are blunter — a single global threshold is wrong "if an image has different lighting conditions in different areas," whereas adaptive thresholding "determines the threshold for a pixel based on a small region around it... which gives better results for images with varying illumination." Why it bites: a phone-photo of a print, a folded blueprint, or a faded copy has a gradient background; one global cutoff drops faint text in the dark corner or floods the bright corner. Expert avoidance: prefer adaptive/local thresholding (or per-tile Otsu) for field-captured drawings, and treat "uneven background darkness" as a known regime, not an edge case. [confirmed via [Tesseract OCR — Improve Quality](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html) and [OpenCV — Image Thresholding tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d7/d4d/tutorial_py_thresholding.html)]

- **Skew and dark scan borders manufacture phantom characters and break line segmentation.** "The quality of Tesseract's line segmentation reduces significantly if a page is too skewed, which severely impacts the quality of the OCR," and dark borders "can be erroneously picked up as extra characters, especially if they vary in shape and gradation." Why it bites: a scanned-at-an-angle or border-shadowed drawing yields mis-segmented rows and spurious glyphs that corrupt dimension strings (e.g. a border smudge read as a digit). Expert avoidance: deskew and crop/clean borders as mandatory preprocessing before recognition — "preprocessing matters more than model modification." [confirmed via [Tesseract OCR — Improve Quality](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html)]

## Verification (proving an extracted read is trustworthy)

- **A multi-read consensus concentrates noisy individual OCR/VLM reads toward a stable value — quantify agreement, don't trust a single pass.** (Theory grounding is in `blueprint-vision-foundations.md` via the Central Limit Theorem; the *practice* is: run independent reads and treat dispersion as the uncertainty estimate.) Why it matters here: the failure modes above (under-resolution, uneven lighting, skew, hand-marks) are *stochastic* — a single pass that happens to land on a smudge fails silently. Expert avoidance: corroborate each dimension across independent reads; a value agreed by ≥2 independent reads is corroborated, a 1-of-N value is a hallucination candidate, and the spread of agreeing reads is a principled per-dimension confidence. (This galaxy's multi-VLM consensus stage implements exactly this; specific agreement thresholds are owner-gated.) [grounded in `blueprint-vision-foundations.md` (OpenStax Central Limit Theorem citation) — no new numeric claim asserted]
