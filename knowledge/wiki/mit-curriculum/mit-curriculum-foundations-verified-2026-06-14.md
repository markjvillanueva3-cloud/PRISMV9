## Synthesis
MIT's manufacturing engineering curriculum is organized as a three-tier ladder: 2.008 (undergraduate process physics and DFM), 2.810/2.830J (graduate process-physics depth plus statistical process control), and 2.72 (machine-element design with mastery-gated fabrication), each anchored by intensive lab work and a semester-long group fabrication project as the capstone pedagogical commitment. The defining pedagogical pattern across all three courses is the synthesis-modeling-fabricating-testing cycle — students must demonstrate theoretical understanding before receiving physical materials, encoding the principle that engineering models are idealizations verified only by physical embodiment. MIT OCW publishes these materials under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with no enrollment requirements, providing free permanent access to lecture notes, problem sets, exams, and lab guides — a structure that makes the full taxonomy of course-material types (lecture/lab/assignment/exam/project) globally accessible. ABET accreditation criteria (led by the Society of Manufacturing Engineers) define the external normative framework that MIT's curriculum architecture must satisfy: five mandatory knowledge areas (materials and processes, process/assembly/product engineering, manufacturing competitiveness, systems with statistical methods, lab measurement), seven universal student outcomes, and a capstone design requirement — confirming that the MIT curriculum taxonomy is both internally coherent and aligned to the profession's authoritative accreditation standard.

## Verified sources
### [2.008 Design and Manufacturing II (Spring 2025) — MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-008-design-and-manufacturing-ii-spring-2025/pages/syllabus/) -- course
> "Course introduces modern manufacturing through four integrated focus areas: manufacturing processes, equipment/control, systems, and design for manufacturing; lab-intensive with 5 hours/week hands-on, two closed-book quizzes, and group project."

**Knowledge:** Canonical MIT undergraduate manufacturing curriculum taxonomy: process physics, variation analysis, equipment control, systems engineering, and DFM as the five pedagogical pillars. Assessment structure: 40% quizzes, 40% lab/reports, 15% homework, 5% participation. Lab-intensive model (5 hours/week) is the defining pedagogical commitment.

### [2.72 Elements of Mechanical Design (Spring 2009) — MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/pages/syllabus/) -- course
> "Advanced course on modeling, design, integration and best practices for use of machine elements such as bearings, springs, gears, cams and mechanisms; students must pass relevant quizzes (>80%) before accessing materials for specific fabrication tasks."

**Knowledge:** Pedagogical model: synthesis-modeling-fabricating-testing cycle with mastery-gated lab access (students demonstrate understanding of shaft design before receiving shaft material). Project-based: teams design, build, and characterize a desktop lathe. Emphasizes that engineering models are idealizations — only the physical embodiment is the full model.

### [2.810 Manufacturing Processes and Systems — MIT Course Page](https://web.mit.edu/2.810/www/) -- course
> "Graduate course introduces manufacturing processes and manufacturing systems including assembly, machining, injection molding, casting, thermoforming; emphasis on physics and randomness of manufacturing and how they influence quality, rate, cost, and design flexibility."

**Knowledge:** Graduate-level extension of 2.008: systems-oriented perspective examining the relationship between process, system, and part design. Two quizzes (60%), semester-long group product-fabrication capstone (30%). Demonstrates MIT's design-through-manufacturing continuity from undergraduate to graduate curriculum.

### [2.830J Control of Manufacturing Processes (SMA 6303, Spring 2008) — MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-830j-control-of-manufacturing-processes-sma-6303-spring-2008/) -- course
> "Course focuses on statistical modeling and control in manufacturing processes including experimental design, response surface modeling, defect and parametric yield modeling, and real-time feedback control with applications in semiconductor, metal/polymer, and micro-nano manufacturing."

**Knowledge:** Third tier in MIT's manufacturing curriculum: statistical process control, run-by-run control, adaptive control, and real-time feedback as the four process-control modalities. Bridging mechanical, electrical, and systems engineering perspectives — illustrating MIT's cross-disciplinary curriculum architecture for manufacturing quality and yield.
