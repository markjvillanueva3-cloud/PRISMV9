## Theme A — Multi-pass sequencing as a precision *strategy* (not just "do skims")

### 1. Roughing owns the recast; skim passes are a deliberate surface-integrity *and* accuracy strategy
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [PMC8839225 — Reviewing Performance Measures of Die-Sinking EDM](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8839225/)):** experts plan "multiple trim-cut strategies to employ minimum surface damage" — the rough pass intentionally absorbs the recast/HAZ while removing bulk, and a *sequence* of trim cuts is what reclaims surface integrity, not a single finishing operation. **When an expert uses it:** any precision or fatigue-critical feature where the rough-cut recast/microcrack layer is unacceptable on the final face. **Trade-off direction:** more/lighter trim passes → better surface integrity and dimensional accuracy, but lower throughput (each trim removes less and adds cycle time). **PRISM galaxy application:** `EDMMultiPassStrategyEngine` should model the pass *count* as derived from a surface-integrity requirement (recast/microcrack budget), not just a dimensional one — distinct from the basic "rough then skim" already in foundations §4.

### 2. Progressive skim conditioning — each pass faster, lighter, tighter, and tenser
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Modern Machine Shop — Buying a Wire EDM, Part 3](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/buying-a-wire-edm-speed-accuracy-and-finish)):** the expert sequencing is directional and monotone — each successive skim "travels at progressively faster speeds, takes less and less material," and across the finish passes "the tension on the wire is increased, the current is reduced, and the voltage gap narrowed." **When an expert uses it:** to refine the spark and the distance it jumps as the part approaches final size, converging on finish without re-roughing. **Trade-off direction:** later passes move the surface less and less (diminishing return) — so the pass plan is chosen from the finish target, never maximized blindly. **PRISM galaxy application:** the per-pass parameter *trajectory* (tension up / current down / gap narrow / speed up) is the schedule a wedm pass-planner emits per pass family — every *magnitude* gated to `constants.ts` / the ACU E-code tables.

## Theme B — Adaptive / online process control (the autonomy layer)

### 3. Self-tuning adaptive control to hold thermal conditions across varying part height
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Frontiers in Mech. Eng. — Comprehensive review on WEDM](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2024.1322605/full)):** self-tuning adaptive controllers monitor sparking frequency and "identify any inappropriate machining conditions live and use a control strategy to keep the wire from breaking" — because "altering the workpiece thickness while machining causes the thermal density of the wire to rise and eventually cause the wire to break." The controller adjusts discharge current and servo voltage *dynamically* to keep thermal load constant as the cut crosses thickness transitions. **When an expert uses it:** stepped/contoured parts, varying height along the cut, and lights-out running where a human cannot react to a brewing instability. **Trade-off direction:** more autonomy and breakage resistance, but online control "necessitates a lot of calculation, which slows down processing." **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm controller engine should expose *workpiece height profile along the path* as a first-class input and de-rate discharge energy across height transitions — the strategic answer to the applied-practice §1 wire-breakage failure mode.

### 4. Fuzzy-logic gap control where no clean physics model exists
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Frontiers in Mech. Eng. — Comprehensive WEDM review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2024.1322605/full)):** fuzzy-logic controllers "maintain the desired machining operation" with "no complicated mathematical models needed" — used precisely because the spark-gap state is noisy and non-linear and resists a tidy closed-form model. **When an expert uses it:** maintaining gap stability under disturbance (debris transients, flushing variation) where a deterministic servo law over- or under-reacts. **Trade-off direction:** robust, model-free stability vs. less interpretable behavior than a tuned PID. **PRISM galaxy application:** PRISM should treat gap-state stabilization as a *control-policy* slot (fuzzy / self-tuning) layered over the discharge-energy setpoint, not as a fixed feed law — and R5 still holds (the routing/threshold logic is code, the policy is the engine).

### 5. Online gap monitoring to compensate progressive wear / drift mid-cut
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [PMC6470516 — Advanced EDM of Stainless Steels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6470516/)):** "online gap monitoring enables real-time compensation... maintaining dimensional accuracy across extended machining cycles without manual intervention." **When an expert uses it:** long, slow precision cuts where small drifts accumulate into out-of-tolerance over hours. **Trade-off direction:** holds accuracy over a long cut at the cost of monitoring overhead. **PRISM galaxy application:** pairs with the foundations §13 polarity/TWR framing — a wedm quality engine should treat the gap signal as a *feedback channel for dimensional drift*, not only an energy setpoint. This is the strategic complement to applied-practice §13 (thermal-stability environment control): one controls the *machine/room*, this controls the *gap*.

## Theme C — Corner strategy: fighting wire-lag error (the signature WEDM accuracy battle)

### 6. Wire lag rounds corners — the expert mitigation is path + energy + pass strategy, not a single knob
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Frontiers in Mech. Eng. — Comprehensive WEDM review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2024.1322605/full)):** the continuous wire electrode "experiences mechanical flex during direction changes," so wire lag/deflection causes "rounded corners and dimensional deviation at sharp edges." The confirmed mitigation *direction* is multi-pronged: (a) trim passes refine corner geometry after the rough removal; (b) lower feed rate and reduced discharge energy favor better corner definition; (c) wire-tension management "prevents drag that exacerbates corner deflection"; (d) appropriate flushing/wire-speed maintain a straighter path. **When an expert uses it:** sharp internal/external corners and tight radii on dies and punches where a rounded corner is a reject. **Trade-off direction:** every corner-accuracy lever (slow down, lower energy, more trim passes, more tension) costs throughput or raises breakage risk — so it is applied *selectively at the corners*, not globally. **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm path/strategy engine should mark corner segments as a distinct regime (feed-down + energy-down + extra-trim) rather than apply uniform pass parameters — a corner-aware pass plan, with all magnitudes gated.

### 7. Wire diameter sets the minimum achievable inside radius — a geometry-feasibility strategy
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Xometry — Wire EDM Machining](https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/wire-edm-machining/)):** the minimum internal radius is "limited by the wire diameter," so the choice of wire gauge is itself a corner-capability decision — a thinner wire reaches sharper inside corners but is more deflection-prone (and breakage-prone). **When an expert uses it:** up-front feasibility on parts with small internal radii — selecting wire before programming, not after. **Trade-off direction:** thinner wire → sharper corners possible but lower stability/robustness and higher breakage risk; thicker wire → robust/fast but a floor on inside-radius sharpness. **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm feasibility/triage engine should gate "can this inside radius be cut?" on the wire-diameter regime *before* path planning — the radius floor is a function of wire choice, and the exact diameters are owner-gated to the JM Die machine/wire tables.

## Theme D — 4-axis taper & independent UV strategy

### 8. Independent upper-guide z-u-v motion for taper and transitioning (top != bottom) shapes
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Wikipedia — Electrical discharge machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_discharge_machining)):** beyond simple tapers, "the upper guide can also move independently in the z-u-v axis, giving rise to the ability to cut tapered *and transitioning* shapes" — i.e. the top profile and the bottom profile can differ (a square-to-round transition, a variable-draft die), driven by decoupling the upper U/V guide from the lower X/Y path. Multi-axis (X-Y-Z-U-V) motion produces tapered profiles and limited 3D contouring ([Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/wire-edm-machining/)). **When an expert uses it:** draft angles on die/mold components and dissimilar top/bottom geometries (transition dies) impossible on a 2-axis cut. **Trade-off direction:** the differential traverse speed top-vs-bottom *re-introduces* the angular-error problem (foundations §5 / applied-practice §12) — the more aggressive the taper or transition, the larger the differential, the more compensation needed. **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm taper engine must model upper (U/V) and lower (X/Y) profiles as *independent* paths and take a test-cut-derived compensation input (never a hardcoded angular constant) — the advanced "transitioning shape" case is two different profiles, not one offset profile.

## Theme E — Recast / white-layer / HAZ minimization as a tuned strategy

### 9. Pulse-off-time is the most sensitive lever on oxide/recast formation — tune it first for surface integrity
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [Frontiers in Mech. Eng. — Comprehensive WEDM review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2024.1322605/full)):** "pulse off time is the most sensitive parameter that influences the formation of [the] layer consisting of [a] mixture of oxides. With a lower value of pulse off time, a considerable reduction in the formation of oxides can be obtained" — while peak current and pulse-on-time "are the driving factors in determining average recast layer thickness." **When an expert uses it:** minimizing white-layer/oxide on the final face of corrosion- or fatigue-critical parts. **Trade-off direction:** the recast-minimizing direction (lower current, lower pulse-on, and the *tuned* pulse-off) reduces material removal rate and cutting speed — surface integrity is bought with time. **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm surface-integrity engine should rank pulse-off as a high-leverage knob for the {recast, white-layer, oxide} quality vector — direction promoted, every magnitude gated to `constants.ts`.

### 10. Modified pulse-generator / finer controlled pulses to break the finish-vs-MRR tension
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [PMC6470516 — Advanced EDM of Stainless Steels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6470516/)):** a "modified iso-duration current pulse generator produced a better surface quality with a higher MRR" by enabling smaller, controlled pulses in finishing passes — a generator-technology strategy that *partially escapes* the usual finish-vs-throughput trade by shaping the pulse rather than just lowering energy. **When an expert uses it:** when the machine offers advanced generator modes and the job demands both fine finish and reasonable speed. **Trade-off direction:** controlled fine pulses reduce recast/microcrack density in finishing while recovering some MRR vs. naive low-energy sparking — the trade-off is *softened*, not eliminated. **PRISM galaxy application:** PRISM should model generator/pulse-shaping *mode* as a categorical strategy input (it changes the achievable finish-vs-MRR frontier), feeding the same gated magnitudes.

### 11. Polarity as a deliberate accuracy-vs-throughput strategy choice
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [PMC6470516 — Advanced EDM of Stainless Steels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6470516/)):** polarity is a strategy axis, not a fixed setting — positive tool polarity minimizes electrode wear (favoring precision), while negative tool polarity favors higher MRR over precision; in wire-EDM the continuous wire feed "mitigates wear concerns," which itself frees the polarity choice toward throughput. **When an expert uses it:** matching the discharge polarity to whether the job is precision-bound or throughput-bound. **Trade-off direction:** positive → less wear / more precision / less speed; negative → more speed / less precision. **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm planner should expose polarity as an explicit accuracy-vs-throughput knob (foundations §13 carries the definition; this is the *when-to-use* strategy), with the wire-feed mitigation noted.

## Theme F — Flushing as a geometry-adaptive strategy (not a fixed setting)

### 12. Match the flushing method to part height/geometry, and intensify flushing as height grows
**Technique (CONFIRMED, [PMC6909068 — Systematic review on powder-mixed EDM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6909068/) + [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/wire-edm-machining/)):** flushing strategy is geometry-dependent — "for deep or tall cavities, traditional side flushing becomes ineffective due to debris accumulation," so "taller setups demand enhanced flushing intensity to prevent short-circuiting," while thin/shallow sections tolerate simpler flushing; thicker sections also "require slower speeds." The named method set (side / suction-through / pressure-through / jet / vacuum / injection flushing) plus servo-controlled reciprocation / vibrating-electrode "hydraulic pumping action" are the levers; even cut *orientation* helps ("when the machining axis is made horizontal, debris evacuation and erosion efficiency improve"). **When an expert uses it:** tall parts, deep slots, and varying-height profiles where uniform flushing starves the gap mid-cut. **Trade-off direction:** taller/deeper → more aggressive flushing + slower speed needed to keep the gap clean; under-flushing → debris → arcing → dimensional + surface loss (applied-practice §2/§3). **PRISM galaxy application:** a wedm planner should carry flushing *mode* and a height-keyed flushing/speed schedule as first-class inputs — the strategic, height-adaptive form of the foundations §10 flushing taxonomy and the applied-practice §3 flushing-mode decision.
