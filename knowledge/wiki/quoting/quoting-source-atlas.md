# Quoting Open-Source Atlas

A curated, **verified**, auto-invokable directory of the best **free + legal LIVING resources** for manufacturing **cost estimation / job-order costing / quoting** — the galaxy's non-stagnant "keep-learning" curriculum. Because every entry points at a continuously-updated homepage (a full course, a textbook that gets new editions, a government data portal, a standards body), this atlas stays current as those sources update, instead of freezing a snapshot of facts.

**How this differs from the rest of the quoting knowledge base (R8 — no duplication):**
- `knowledge/wiki/quoting/quoting-foundations.md` cites *specific page-level primary sources* to back *individual asserted facts* (e.g. OpenStax §4.4 for the predetermined-overhead-rate formula). **This atlas does NOT repeat those page-level citations** — it curates the *broader living homepages* those pages live inside (the whole OpenStax book, the whole MIT course, the standards body) plus net-new living sources not in foundations at all (LibreTexts, archive.org, Census ASM, BEA, edX, ASME C&S, OSHA, MIT 15.501, NIST Manufacturing-Economics landing, NIST MCG tool).
- `state/shared/specs/GALAXY-FREE-SOURCE-CORPUS-2026-06-09.md` is a bulk flat pointer list (11 quoting entries, NOT auto-invoked, NOT individually re-verified). **This atlas is the curated + re-verified + per-galaxy form** — the strongest ~15 continuously-updated sources, each WebFetch-confirmed on 2026-06-10 and organized by type.

> **R12 honesty note:** a verified link only means the resource is real, free, and on-topic — it does **not** make any dollar rate, percentage, or constant inside it "verified." Pricing/rate constants stay owner-gated for charlie (see `quoting-foundations.md` §Owner-gate). ## Free college courses

Full course / lecture-series homepages — free, openly licensed, and maintained.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 15.501 — Introduction to Financial and Managerial Accounting** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-501-introduction-to-financial-and-managerial-accounting-spring-2004/ — Sloan undergrad accounting course (Prof. Roychowdhury), CC-licensed. The cost-*accounting* foundation behind quoting: how a job accumulates direct material, direct labor, and overhead before a sales price is set. Net-new vs foundations (foundations cites no 15.501).
- **MIT OCW 15.501 — Lecture Notes (26 sessions + exams)** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-501-introduction-to-financial-and-managerial-accounting-spring-2004/pages/lecture-notes/ — the downloadable per-lecture PDFs (balance sheet → cost recording → managerial decisions → wrap-up). A free, self-paced curriculum charlie can hand to anyone onboarding the quoting domain.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare 2.854 — Introduction to Manufacturing Systems** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-854-introduction-to-manufacturing-systems-fall-2016/ — graduate manufacturing-systems course (Dr. Gershwin); queueing, inventory, scheduling, quality/quantity interaction. Why a quote's promised *lead time* and *cost* are outputs of a stochastic, queue-governed shop — the capacity-aware-quoting spine. (Atlas lists the *course homepage*; foundations cited only the lecture-notes subpage.)
- **edX — Accounting course catalog** — https://www.edx.org/learn/accounting — continuously-refreshed catalog of accounting courses (63+ courses, many auditable for free) from universities and institutions. A living source of fresh managerial-/cost-accounting MOOCs that stays current as new courses launch.

## Free textbooks & references

Open-license textbook homepages — free to read, periodically revised, the durable reference spine.

- **OpenStax — Principles of Accounting, Vol. 2: Managerial Accounting** — https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/1-1-define-managerial-accounting-and-identify-the-three-primary-responsibilities-of-management — the full free CC-BY-NC-SA textbook (Rice University / OpenStax). Job-order costing, the three product-cost components, predetermined overhead rates, ABC, cost behavior / NRE amortization, standard costing — the canonical quoting-methodology reference. (Atlas points at the book's chapter-1 entry as the living homepage; foundations cites individual mid-book sections for specific facts.)
- **LibreTexts (Business) — Managerial Accounting** — https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Accounting/Managerial_Accounting — a *second, independent* free OER managerial-accounting textbook (CC-BY-NC-SA, 13 chapters: job costing, ABC, cost behavior, budgeting, variance analysis). Net-new vs foundations — a cross-check source so the galaxy isn't single-textbook-dependent.
- **Internet Archive — Texts / Books collection** — https://archive.org/details/texts — free + borrowable digital library incl. public-domain accounting, cost-accounting, and industrial-engineering classics. The long-tail living archive for older estimation/costing texts (e.g. early job-costing and shop-management works) that have aged into the public domain.

## Archives & open data / gov reports

Continuously-updated government data portals and free cost-estimation tools — the "data reports" backbone for benchmarking JM Die's internal rates against published reality.

- **NIST — Manufacturing Cost Guide (MCG) tool** — https://www.nist.gov/services-resources/software/mcg-supply-chain-statistics — free, public-domain Excel/macro tool (Douglas Thomas, NIST Applied Economics Office) that estimates value-added, labor compensation, energy, and supply-chain costs by NAICS/SOC. A government-grade external sanity-check for shop-rate and material-cost assumptions in a quote.
- **NIST — Manufacturing Economics (Applied Economics Office)** — https://www.nist.gov/manufacturing-economics — the program homepage producing annual U.S. manufacturing-economy statistics, cost-mapping tools, and capital-investment / decision-making guides. The living index to NIST's cost-economics publications as new ones land.
- **U.S. Census Bureau — Annual Survey of Manufactures (ASM) / Annual Integrated Economic Survey (AIES)** — https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/asm.html — establishment-level manufacturing statistics (value of shipments, cost of materials, payroll, value added) by industry. The authoritative public baseline for "what does this industry actually spend on materials vs labor" — calibration data for cost-decomposition assumptions. (Note on page: ASM transitioned into the AIES starting 2024.)
- **U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis — Industry Economic Accounts** — https://www.bea.gov/industry — GDP-by-Industry, Gross Output by Industry, and Input-Output Accounts (free). Lets a quoting model situate a part's industry within sector-level cost/output structure and supplier interdependence — supply-chain-aware quoting context.

## Standards & authoritative bodies

Official standards-body landing pages — the durable, version-controlled authority surfaces relevant to costing, quality-cost, and the safety/compliance line items a quote must absorb.

- **ASME — Codes & Standards** — https://www.asme.org/codes-standards — the official ASME standards catalog landing page (GD&T Y14.5, pressure tech, etc.). Drawing/tolerance and inspection standards drive the cost-of-quality and tolerance-cost line items a quote must price; this is the living entry point to the current editions.
- **OSHA — Laws & Regulations** — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs — the U.S. Occupational Safety and Health Administration standards/regulations landing page (General Industry, Construction, etc.). Compliance/PPE/safety overhead is a real burden component in a defensible shop rate; OSHA is the authoritative, continuously-updated source for what that regulatory cost floor actually is.
