# Tribal-Knowledge Open Source Atlas

The **living-source curriculum** for the **tribal-knowledge** galaxy: a curated directory of *where to keep learning* the knowledge-management and organizational-learning domain from reputable, free/legal sources — so the galaxy's understanding never goes stagnant.

This entry is deliberately **distinct** from its two siblings:
- [`tribal-knowledge-foundations.md`](tribal-knowledge-foundations.md) is the **synthesized theory** (SECI, tacit/explicit, single/double-loop, DeLong retention, communities of practice).
- [`tribal-knowledge-applied-practice.md`](tribal-knowledge-applied-practice.md) is the **practitioner gotchas** (externalization loss, write-only memory, SSOT drift, recall miss, stale tips).
- **This atlas** is the **keep-learning directory** — the free courses, free textbooks, open KM resources, primary-author theory pages, lecture material, and gov reports a maintainer revisits to refresh the galaxy. It does NOT re-teach the theory or re-list the gotchas; it points at *where to go learn more, free, forever.*

## 1. Free college courses (full open courseware)

Complete, free, no-registration courses that teach the people-and-organizations / management-of-knowledge substrate this galaxy models.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 15.668 "People and Organizations"** (Sloan School; Prof. Thomas A. Kochan) — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-668-people-and-organizations-fall-2010/
  - *Teaches:* the historical + current human and organizational contexts in which professionals work — organizational behavior, HR, leadership, teamwork, negotiation — via interactive exercises and simulations. Free, no registration; lecture notes + readings + syllabus available.
  - *Feeds:* the galaxy's organizational-learning + knowledge-transfer frame (foundations §5–6). The structured, citable corpus PRISM grounds its "how does know-how move through a group" reasoning against.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 15.279 "Management Communication for Undergraduates"** (Sloan School; Dr. Lori Breslow, Dr. Terence Heagney) — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-279-management-communication-for-undergraduates-fall-2012/
  - *Teaches:* strategic professional communication — analyzing audience, purpose, and context before crafting a message; writing, speaking, teamwork, cross-cultural communication. Free; lecture notes + materials (notes-only, no video).
  - *Feeds:* the **externalization edge** (applied-practice §1) — articulating tacit know-how into a tip a reader can act on is fundamentally a communication problem; this course is the craft-of-articulation reference.

## 2. Free textbooks (openly licensed, free to read/download)

- **OpenStax "Organizational Behavior"** (Rice University nonprofit; J. Stewart Black & David S. Bright; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — https://openstax.org/books/organizational-behavior/pages/1-introduction
  - *Teaches:* how individuals and groups affect organizational performance — motivation, learning & reinforcement, teams, leadership, organizational structure & change. Free web view + PDF.
  - *Feeds:* the management-science backbone behind the galaxy's organizational-memory and learning-organization model (foundations §5).

- **University of Minnesota Libraries "Organizational Behavior" open textbook** (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — https://open.lib.umn.edu/organizationalbehavior/
  - *Teaches:* an "experiential" theory-to-practice bridge for organizational behavior. Freely readable online (alternate versions via Saylor / LibreTexts).
  - *Feeds:* a second independent OER framing of the same substrate — useful for cross-checking a claim before it earns a tribal tip (anti-SSOT-drift discipline, applied-practice §2.1).

- **Open Textbook Library — Management subject** (University of Minnesota; peer-reviewed, CC-licensed) — https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/subjects/management
  - *Teaches:* a curated catalog of 60 free management/leadership/strategy textbooks (the directory itself, not one book).
  - *Feeds:* the galaxy's **keep-fresh source pool** — when a cited textbook ages out, this catalog is where a maintainer finds the current free replacement. A living index, not a frozen link.

## 3. Knowledge-management open resources

- **Open University OpenLearn — "Digital Knowledge Management"** (free course section, part of "Hybrid working: skills for digital transformation") — https://www.open.edu/openlearn/digital-computing/hybrid-working-skills-digital-transformation/content-section-5.6.1
  - *Teaches:* DKM as "the process of identifying, collecting, documenting, organising and storing digital information and knowledge so that everyone, including computer-automated systems, can find and access" it — file storage, metadata, naming, governance, backup. Completely free, no payment.
  - *Feeds:* the **storage + retrieval edges** (applied-practice §2–3) — this is the operational KM-hygiene curriculum that PRISM's `tribal-embed-index` / `tribal-by-domain-inject` surfaces implement, stated as teachable practice.

- **Wenger-Trayner — "Introduction to Communities of Practice"** (Etienne & Beverly Wenger-Trayner; CC 4.0; PDF EN/ES) — https://www.wenger-trayner.com/introduction-to-communities-of-practice/
  - *Teaches:* the **primary-author** definition of communities of practice — Domain, Community, Practice — origins, applications across sectors, and common misconceptions. Freely readable + downloadable PDF.
  - *Feeds:* foundations §4. Both siblings cite CoP only via Wikipedia; this is the source-of-record from the theory's own author, so the galaxy can ground its 34-galaxy "community-of-practice federation" framing on the originator, not a tertiary summary.

## 4. Official docs & gov reports (institutional-scale validation)

- **US GAO-05-585 "Human Capital: Selected Agencies Have Opportunities to Enhance Existing Succession Planning and Management Efforts"** (US Government Accountability Office, 2005) — https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-05-585
  - *Teaches:* federal-scale evidence that organizations must "go beyond a succession planning approach that focuses on replacing individuals" toward "strengthening current and future organizational capacity"; monitoring mission-critical workforce gaps; coordinating training to reduce duplication. Public, free.
  - *Feeds:* foundations §6 + applied-practice §2.3 — the documented, audited proof that **knowledge retention is a measured operational risk**, not a soft nicety. The federal analogue of "retain the slot's knowledge before /compact closes the window."

## Keep-fresh cadence

A living-source atlas rots the moment a link 404s or a course is archived (applied-practice §4.1 — software/documentation rot). To keep this entry trustworthy:

- **Re-verify every URL on a ~90-day horizon.** Open courseware and OER catalogs reorganize URLs routinely (this very build dropped 4 OCW/OpenLearn links that had 404'd since the siblings were written). A link unconfirmed past 90 days is treated as STALE, not trusted because it's in the list.
- **Prefer catalog/index destinations over single deep links.** The Open Textbook Library management subject (§2) and OpenLearn's course tree survive reorganization far better than a single lecture-notes deep link — they self-heal as the underlying works move. When a single-book link dies, the catalog is where the replacement is found.
- **Re-run the WebFetch confirmation, never hand-edit a URL to "fix" it.** If a fetch fails, retry once then DROP — adding a guessed-corrected URL is the exact fabrication R12 forbids. A dropped source is honest; a hallucinated one is a landmine.
- **A lecture-VIDEO destination is a known gap.** WebFetch cannot confirm YouTube channel pages (they return footer chrome) and the OCW video galleries probed here redirect-looped; no video source is listed rather than list an unconfirmed one. A future maintainer with a confirmable video index should add a §"Lecture-video channels" section then.
- **Promotion path:** when a source here proves repeatedly useful, the *content* learned from it flows into foundations (theory) or applied-practice (gotchas) — this atlas stays a pure directory and never absorbs the lessons it points at.
