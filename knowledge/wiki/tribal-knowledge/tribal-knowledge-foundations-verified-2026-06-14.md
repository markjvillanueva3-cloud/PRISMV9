## Synthesis
The deepest next-layer knowledge in expert tacit-knowledge capture centers on four compounding pillars. First, Cognitive Task Analysis — particularly the Critical Decision Method (Klein, Calderwood & MacGregor 1989) — provides the only validated mechanism for reaching knowledge experts cannot voluntarily produce: structured retrospective probing on nonroutine incidents surfaces cue-recognition patterns, situation-assessment heuristics, and typicality judgments that direct questioning systematically misses. Second, the empirical record (Sporsem et al. 2023; Swaby et al. 2022 systematic review of 81 studies) convergently demonstrates that passive interview alone leaves the most critical 'unknowns' inaccessible — extended field observation combined with multi-pass incident retrospection is necessary for full coverage. Third, the CommonKADS methodology provides the engineering complement: once elicited, expert knowledge must be modeled into validated, reusable knowledge artifacts (task models, domain models, inference models) rather than left as unstructured narrative, enabling knowledge-intensive systems to be built on a proven foundation. Fourth, ISO 30401:2018 gives organizations the governance scaffold to institutionalize these practices at scale with auditable requirements for capture, storage, and transfer — closing the loop from individual elicitation session to organizational knowledge asset. Taken together, the literature establishes that tribal knowledge codification is not a documentation problem but a cognitive-engineering problem requiring purpose-built elicitation instruments, structured representation models, and systematic organizational governance.

> "While observational techniques are costly to use, we conclude that essential information is uncovered, which is key for developers to understand system users and their concerns."

**Knowledge:** Ethnographic field observation (80+ hours) combined with semi-structured interviews systematically surfaces tacit knowledge invisible to traditional requirements elicitation; multi-method triangulation (observation + interview + diary) is necessary because neither technique alone reaches the 'unknowns' experts cannot voluntarily articulate.
