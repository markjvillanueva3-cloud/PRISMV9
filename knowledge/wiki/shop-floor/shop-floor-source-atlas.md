# Shop-Floor Open Source Atlas

A curated, **verified** directory of the best FREE + LEGAL **living** (continuously-updated) resources for the shop-floor galaxy — shop-floor operations, OEE, MES, lean, and industrial safety. The point of this atlas is a **non-stagnant "keep-learning" curriculum**: it points at sources that stay current on their own (course homepages that get new terms each year, standards-body landing pages, gov data/program hubs, browsable glossaries, video channels), so the galaxy's knowledge does not rot.

**How this differs from its siblings (R8 — no duplication):**
- `knowledge/wiki/shop-floor/shop-floor-foundations.md` extracts *specific verified facts* from individual pages (the MTConnect-is-read-only fact, the OEE = A×P×Q formula, etc.). This atlas instead curates the **broader living homepages / course series / glossaries / portals** those facts can be re-derived from — the directory, not the extracted claims.
- `state/shared/specs/GALAXY-FREE-SOURCE-CORPUS-2026-06-09.md` is a flat, machine-generated, *not-auto-invoked* pointer dump (21 shop-floor entries, many of them single ISO/blog pages). This atlas is the **curated + re-verified + type-organized + auto-invokable** per-galaxy form: ~16 of the strongest *living* sources, each fetch-confirmed, organized by type.

Where a source also appears in a sibling, it earns its place here only by being a **living index/homepage** (e.g. the Lean Lexicon *glossary listing* vs. the foundations' single Heijunka-Box term page; the OSHA Machine-Guarding *eTool* vs. the foundations' plain topic page; the MIT OCW *homepage* as the continuously-updated course index).

## Free college courses

Full course homepages / OpenCourseWare — the living lecture-series spine for production-systems and operations theory.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare (homepage / living index)** — https://ocw.mit.edu/ — The continuously-updated free index of 2,500+ MIT courses ("free lecture notes, exams, and videos, no registration"). Confirmed live with Spring-2026 / IAP-2026 courses featured. The canonical entry point to keep finding fresh manufacturing-systems, operations, and quality courseware over time.
- **MIT OCW 2.830J — Control of Manufacturing Processes** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-830j-control-of-manufacturing-processes-sma-6303-spring-2008/ — Graduate course (Hardt, Boning) on statistical modeling and control of manufacturing processes: SPC, run-by-run / adaptive control, real-time feedback, yield modeling. Directly relevant to the "Quality" leg of OEE and to MES quality operations. Carries video lectures.
- **MIT OCW 15.760A — Operations Management (Sloan)** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-760a-operations-management-spring-2002/ — Business-school treatment (Charles H. Fine): operations strategy, inventory, process technology/ERP, queueing, process quality/TQM, Toyota Production System, theory of constraints, supply-chain. Frames the shop floor inside the firm's operations strategy (ISA-95 Level-4 context). Lecture notes available.
- **MIT 2.810 — Manufacturing Processes and Systems (course homepage)** — https://web.mit.edu/2.810/www/ — The live MIT MechE course site (Gutowski): manufacturing processes, the process↔system relationship, rate/cost/quality/flexibility. A continuously-re-offered course whose homepage is updated each term — good for the "systems" half of shop-floor operations.

## Free textbooks & references

Openly-licensed textbook homepages and browsable living glossaries.

- **OpenStax — Principles of Management (free, CC-licensed)** — https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/1-introduction — Rice/OpenStax free college textbook (Creative Commons). Covers management fundamentals, the history of management (scientific management, human relations), and operations — the conceptual backbone for shop-floor supervision, work-measurement, and lean's organizational roots. Always free, no registration.
- **Lean Enterprise Institute — Lean Lexicon (online glossary)** — https://www.lean.org/explore-lean/lexicon-terms/ — The authoritative browsable lean glossary: 200+ terms A→Z (kanban, kaizen, takt time, jidoka, heijunka, standardized work, value-stream mapping, 5S, …), each linking to its own definition page, with a search box. The living reference for lean vocabulary on the shop floor (the corpus only pointed at the single Heijunka-Box term page — this is the whole, maintained dictionary).

## Archives & open data / gov reports

Government program hubs and a free digital library — the "data reports" + continuously-published authoritative material.

- **NIST — Manufacturing (topic landing hub)** — https://www.nist.gov/manufacturing — NIST's manufacturing hub: 16 research topics (additive, factory communications, robotics, supply chain, sustainable manufacturing), plus news, blog, publications and patents. The continuously-updated front door to NIST's free manufacturing research and reports.
- **NIST — Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP)** — https://www.nist.gov/mep — The public-private SMB-manufacturer support network (≈1,400 experts, 450+ locations): value-stream mapping, Industry-4.0 adoption, workforce development, plus success stories, infographics, impact data and a manufacturing blog. A living source of shop-floor operational-improvement reports.
- **NIST — Smart Manufacturing Operations Planning & Control Program** — https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/smart-manufacturing-operations-planning-and-control-program — The NIST program page defining "smart manufacturing" (real-time-integrated factory + supply-network + customer demand) and the "digital thread." Foundational reference for MES/traceability and real-time shop-floor control. (Program concluded 2018; the definitions/digital-thread framing remain the citable artifact, with follow-on work under the Model-Based Enterprise program.)
- **NIST — Baldrige Performance Excellence Program** — https://www.nist.gov/baldrige — The Baldrige Excellence Framework + criteria + self-assessment tools, with an explicit **Manufacturing** sector track. A free, maintained organizational-maturity / performance-excellence scorecard the shop-floor galaxy can lean on instead of inventing an ad-hoc maturity scale.
- **Internet Archive — Texts (free digital library)** — https://archive.org/details/texts — The browsable free library of public-domain and openly-licensed books/documents — a deep, continuously-growing well of out-of-copyright industrial-engineering, work-study, and management texts (Taylor, Gilbreth, early TPS literature) for the "keep-learning" reading list.

## Standards & authoritative bodies

Living standards-organization landing pages relevant to shop-floor operations / OEE / MES / lean / industrial safety.

- **MTConnect Institute (homepage)** — https://www.mtconnect.org/ — The body behind the MTConnect machine-data standard (ANSI/MTC1.4): a domain-specific semantic vocabulary for reading manufacturing-equipment data ("structured, contextualized data, no proprietary format"). Getting-started, docs, GitHub, and community — the authoritative living home for the machine-monitoring standard that feeds OEE.
- **ISA — ISA-95 Standards Committee (Enterprise-Control System Integration)** — https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-standards-committees/isa95 — The International Society of Automation's official ISA-95 committee page: the standard defining the interface between control functions and enterprise functions (the ERP↔MES↔control hierarchy the shop floor lives at, Level 3). The authoritative landing page, kept current by the committee.
- **OSHA — Machine Guarding eTool** — https://www.osha.gov/etools/machine-guarding — OSHA's interactive machine-safeguarding training tool (saws, presses, plastics machinery; ~18,000 amputations + 800 deaths/yr context). A maintained, hands-on industrial-safety reference — richer than the plain topic page — for the shop-floor safety surface.
- **OSHA — Control of Hazardous Energy (Lockout/Tagout)** — https://www.osha.gov/control-hazardous-energy — OSHA's LOTO landing page (29 CFR 1910.147): controlling electrical/mechanical/hydraulic/pneumatic/thermal energy during servicing. The authoritative source for the "machine in LOTO / under-maintenance" state every shop-floor machine-status model must represent distinctly from "down."
- **OSHA — Ergonomics** — https://www.osha.gov/ergonomics — OSHA's ergonomics landing page ("fitting a job to a person," preventing musculoskeletal disorders): risk factors, high-risk occupations, and the seven-element prevention process. The authoritative living source for treating worker safety as a first-class shop-floor process metric alongside uptime.
