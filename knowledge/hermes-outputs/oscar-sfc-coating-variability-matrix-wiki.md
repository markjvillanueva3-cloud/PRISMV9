## Overview
Different coatings behave very differently depending on the workpiece material, cutting speed, and operation type. This matrix covers the major commercial coatings across major tool brands.

## Coating Performance by Material & Brand

| Coating          | Major Brands                  | Best On          | kc Reduction | Wear Resistance | Notes / Limitations                          |
|------------------|-------------------------------|------------------|--------------|------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| TiAlN            | Sandvik, Kennametal, OSG, Mitsubishi | P, M, K         | 8–15%       | Very Good       | Excellent at high speeds; oxidizes >800°C   |
| AlCrN            | Sandvik, Seco, Kyocera       | P, M, S         | 10–18%      | Excellent       | Best high-temp performance; brittle         |

## Brand-Specific Coating Notes

- **Sandvik Coromant**: Strong in TiAlN (GC grades) and AlCrN. Very consistent performance data.
- **Kennametal**: Good TiAlN and DLC offerings. Strong in CVD Diamond for graphite.
- **OSG**: Excellent TiSiN and DLC lines. Very good on hardened materials.
- **Mitsubishi**: Strong TiAlN and TiSiN. Good value/performance balance.
- **Seco**: Competitive AlCrN and TiAlN. Often used in automotive.
- **Kyocera**: Good AlCrN and TiAlN. Strong in Asian markets.
- **Tungaloy**: Competitive TiSiN and AlCrN. Often aggressive on pricing.

## Material Interaction Summary

- **P-Steels**: TiAlN or AlCrN preferred. AlCrN wins at very high speeds.
- **M-Stainless**: TiAlN or TiSiN. Watch for built-up edge.
- **K-Cast Iron**: TiAlN or TiSiN. Abrasion resistance critical.
- **N-Aluminum**: DLC or CVD Diamond. TiAlN can work but galls.
- **S-Heat Resistant**: AlCrN or TiSiN. Temperature resistance is key.
- **H-Hardened Steel**: TiSiN or CVD Diamond. Hardness matters most.
