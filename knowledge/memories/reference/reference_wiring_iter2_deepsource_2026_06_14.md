## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Quantitative Resource-Aware Semantics, Synchronous Dataflow, and Verified Hardware-Software Co-Design for Wiring**

A world-leading expert in PRISM’s wiring galaxy next masters the rigorous integration of **quantitative algebraic effects**, **synchronous dataflow languages**, and **verified compilation pipelines** that treat wiring as typed resource flows with timing, latency, and energy budgets.

Core topics include:
- Denotational models for **resource-sensitive dataflow** using graded modal types and quantitative effect systems.
- Clock calculus, polychronous signals, and refinement types for deterministic concurrent wiring (SIGNAL/Polychrony family).
- Certified compilation of dataflow languages to hardware (Lustre → VHDL/Verilog) with end-to-end semantic preservation.
- Algebraic theories of **weighted bisimulation** and **quantitative equational logic** for reasoning about wiring cost and latency.

**Key next sources:**

- **Ghica & Smith (2017)** “Bunched Types for Resource Semirings” (MFPS) – extends their 2014 work to wiring-scale resource composition.
- **Baelde, Doumane, Jaber (2021)** “A Curry-Howard Correspondence for Linear, Reversible Logic” (LICS) – provides foundations for reversible wiring semantics.
- **Pottier (2013)** “A Taste of Dependent Types” and subsequent “Mezzo” type system for controlling aliasing and resource flow in concurrent settings.
- **Lucid Synchrone 4.0 documentation & Colaço et al. (2005)** “A Type-based Analysis of Causality Loops in Hybrid Systems” (HSCC).
- **Boulanger (2010)** “A Synchronous Programming Language for Embedded Systems” (book, Springer) – formal semantics of Lustre/Scade-style wiring.
- **Leroy (2009)** “Formal Verification of a Realistic Compiler” (CACM) – CompCert pipeline applied to dataflow-generated hardware descriptions.
- **IEEE Std 1076.6-2004** (VHDL RTL Synthesis) – standard linking high-level wiring models to gate-level realization.
- **NASA/CR-2010-216724 (2010)** “Formal Methods Case Studies for DO-333” – wiring certification patterns in avionics dataflow.

These build directly on the already-covered Milner/Winskel dataflow foundations, Ghica’s geometry of synthesis, quantitative algebra (Mardare et al.), and program analysis curriculum without repeating them.
