## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: High-Speed Machining Thermal-Mechanical Coupling and Adiabatic Shear Banding at the Tool-Chip Interface**

World-leading mastery in speed-feed requires command of adiabatic shear localization theory under extreme strain-rate and thermal-softening conditions, coupled with advanced friction and heat-partition models that govern tool-chip interface stability at velocities >300 m/min.

**Key uncovered domains:**

- **Adiabatic Shear Band (ASB) formation and critical strain-rate criteria** derived from perturbation analysis of the hyperbolic heat-flow + viscoplastic equations (Wright & Walter, 1987; Molinari et al., 2013).
- **Thermo-viscoplastic instability models** using the Zener-Hollomon parameter and modified Johnson-Cook or Steinberg-Guinan formulations for shear-band spacing and chip segmentation frequency (Molinari, 1997; Recht, 1964 updated by Tounsi et al., 2013).
- **Heat partition coefficient evolution** at the tool-chip interface under sliding-sticking transition using temperature-dependent friction models (including Coulomb + constant shear + temperature-modified Usui models) and moving heat-source theory (Boothroyd, 1963; Shaw, 1980; Komanduri & Hou, 2001 extension).
- **Dynamic recrystallization and microstructural evolution** driving flow softening in the primary and secondary shear zones (Oxley, 1989 advanced by Guo et al., 2011; Rittel et al., 2010).
- **Standards and metrology for high-speed thermal measurement**: ISO 230-11:2016 (test code for machine tools — Part 11: measuring thermomechanical errors) and NIST IR 8014 (2014) on high-speed infrared thermography of cutting zones.

**Advanced FE techniques:** Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) with adaptive remeshing and element deletion for chip segmentation, plus coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) formulations to capture ASB without mesh distortion (LS-DYNA MAT_224, ABAQUS explicit with VUMAT for rate-dependent damage).

These layers extend beyond already-covered Childs, Marusich, Özel, Jaspers, and basic Johnson-Cook/Zerilli-Armstrong models into predictive simulation of serrated chip morphology, tool-face temperature spikes, and thermal error compensation under true high-speed conditions.
