**Next Layer: Advanced Training Systems – Theory, Compilation, and Scaling Laws**

A world-leading expert in ai-training must next master **training dynamics beyond scaling laws**, **compiler-level optimizations for ML**, **memory-efficient algorithmic differentiation**, and **formal verification of training stability**.

Key uncovered areas include:

- **Chinchilla-style optimal compute allocation** extensions using **muP (maximal update parameterization)** and **tensor programs** for hyperparameter transfer across model scales (Yang et al., 2022).
- **ML compilers and automatic differentiation at scale**: polyhedral compilation, operator fusion graphs, and differentiable programming semantics (Paszke et al., 2019; Frostig et al., 2021).
- **Distributed training system architectures**: 3D parallelism, sequence parallelism, and pipeline schedule optimization with zero-bubble techniques (Narayanan et al., 2021; Qi et al., 2023).
- **Theoretical foundations of stable large-batch training**: sharpness-aware minimization, spectral norm analysis of the Hessian, and edge-of-stability phenomena (Foret et al., 2021; Cohen et al., 2021).
- **Governance and risk formalization for training pipelines**: mapping AI RMF to concrete training-run risk registers and red-teaming of training data (NIST, 2023 extensions via SP 800-220 draft).

These build directly on covered works (FlashAttention, ZeRO, FP8, PyTorch 2, MIT 6.8440/6.S898) by shifting from implementation tricks to **principled compiler theory**, **scaling law extrapolation**, and **verifiable training systems**.
