## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Regenerative chatter stability in milling – frequency-domain and time-domain prediction beyond basic semi-discretization, including process damping, variable pitch/helix tools, and stability lobe sensitivity to uncertain parameters.**

**METHOD/THEORY:**

- Analytical construction of stability lobes using the zero-order approximation (ZOA) and multi-frequency solution (MFS) for milling with multiple regenerative delays (Altintas & Budak extensions post-1998).
- Chebyshev collocation and full-discretization methods for periodic DDEs with distributed delays arising from process damping and variable engagement.
- Stability analysis of variable-pitch and variable-helix cutters via clustered semi-discretization or spectral element methods.
- Incorporation of process damping via force models dependent on cutting speed and vibration wavelength; identification from specially designed plunge-turning or milling tests.
- Sensitivity analysis of stability limits to parametric uncertainty (modal parameters, cutting force coefficients) using Monte-Carlo, polynomial chaos, or interval arithmetic.
- Experimental validation protocols per ASME and ISO test pieces, including uncertainty quantification of FRF measurements and force signals.

**STANDARDS & GOVERNMENT SOURCES:**

- ASME B5.54-2005 Methods for Performance Evaluation of Computer Numerically Controlled Machining Centers.
- NIST Technical Note 1831 (2014) – “A Method to Determine the Uncertainty of Machining Force Measurements.”
- NASA/TP-2010-216260 – “Machining and Assembly of Stainless Steel Aerospace Components.”
