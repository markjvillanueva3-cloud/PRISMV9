## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Advanced Parametric and Constraint-Based CAD Theory (Iteration 4)**

A world-leading expert in CAD must next master rigorous algebraic, topological, and declarative foundations for robust parametric modeling, variational solving, and persistent semantic representations beyond basic constraint satisfaction and B-rep deformation.

Key uncovered topics include:
- **Variational geometric modeling using Lie algebras and screw theory** for rigid-body constraint systems.
- **Persistent naming and semantic reference mechanisms** that survive topological perturbations in history-based modeling.
- **Combinatorial rigidity theory and matroid-based decomposition** for solving large geometric constraint systems (GCS).
- **Declarative product definition and feature semantics** per ISO/STEP parameterization schemas.
- **Exact boundary evaluation and non-manifold cellular modeling** under parametric variation.

**Core next-layer sources:**

- **Algebraic and rigidity-theoretic solvers**: Michelucci & Foufou (2004) on rigidity, distance, and angle constraints using Cayley-Menger determinants; Sitharam et al. (2014) already covered, but the deeper combinatorial decomposition via Henneberg sequences and 3D rigidity matroids is required next.
- **Persistent naming formalization**: Raghothama & Shapiro (2000) covered; next is Kripac (1997) formal model and Capoyleas et al. (1996) on naming topological entities in parametric solid modeling.
- **Declarative and STEP-integrated parameterization**: Pratt (2001) on parametric and feature-based CAD in ISO 10303; ISO 10303-108/111 already cited; next depth is ISO 10303-214:2023 (AP242 Edition 2) semantic PMI and parametric representation.
- **Non-manifold variational modeling**: Rossignac & Requicha (1991) on constructive non-regularized geometry; beyond MIT 6.838, the key is Weiler (1988) topological structures for non-manifold modeling.

These provide the institutional (ASME/ISO/NIST), mathematical (rigidity theory, algebraic geometry), and systems depth required for next-generation constraint-based platforms.
