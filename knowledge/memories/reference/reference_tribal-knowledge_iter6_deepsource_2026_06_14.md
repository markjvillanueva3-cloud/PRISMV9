## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Boundary Infrastructures, Epistemic Alignment, and Knowledge Governance in Sociotechnical Systems**

A world-leading expert in PRISM’s tribal-knowledge galaxy must next master the **infrastructural and governance mechanisms** that sustain distributed knowledge work across heterogeneous communities of practice. This layer moves beyond boundary objects and absorptive capacity to examine how classification systems, standards, and architectural knowledge repositories become stable yet mutable infrastructures that enable (and constrain) knowledge flow in large-scale software-intensive organizations.

Core next-tier knowledge includes:
- **Boundary infrastructures** as robust yet revisable systems that allow multiple epistemic communities to cooperate without consensus (Bowker & Star extensions).
- **Infrastructural inversion** and the invisible work of maintaining classification schemes and knowledge standards over time.
- **Knowledge governance** mechanisms: how firms design architectural decision records, viewpoint frameworks, and epistemic contracts to manage transactive memory and prevent epistemic drift.
- **Sociomaterial practices of knowledge versioning** and the role of standards in coordinating epistemic cultures at enterprise scale.
- **Organizational knowledge architectures** that treat knowledge as both product and infrastructure (IEEE/ISO alignment with systems engineering life-cycle knowledge management).

**Key next sources (post the listed canon):**

- Bowker, G.C. (2005) *Memory Practices in the Sciences*. MIT Press. (extends classification infrastructures to memory and knowledge retention regimes)
- Edwards, P.N. et al. (2013) “Knowledge Infrastructures: Intellectual Frameworks and Research Challenges.” University of Michigan Working Paper.
- ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 (Amendment 1) – explicit guidance on architecture viewpoints for knowledge and decision capture.
- Carlile, P.R. & Rebentisch, E.S. (2003) “Into the Black Box: The Knowledge Transformation Cycle.” *Management Science*.
- Monteiro, E. et al. (2013) “From Artefacts to Infrastructures.” *Computer Supported Cooperative Work*.
- NIST Special Publication 800-53 Rev. 5 (2020) – control families on knowledge management, system documentation, and configuration management as governance baselines (software/systems lens only).
- MIT OCW 6.938 (Fall 2019) Engineering Systems Design – knowledge architecture and infrastructure modules.

These provide the institutional, standards-based, and theoretical depth required for scalable tribal-knowledge systems in complex software organizations.
