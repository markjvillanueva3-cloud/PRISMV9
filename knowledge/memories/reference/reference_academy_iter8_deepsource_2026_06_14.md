**NEXT LAYER: Curriculum as a Socio-Technical System – Architectural Decision Records, Pattern Languages, and Cognitive Load Theory in Curriculum Engineering**

A world-leading expert next masters the application of **architectural decision records (ADRs)** and **pattern languages** to curriculum design, treating the curriculum as a living, evolvable socio-technical artifact whose decisions must be explicitly captured, traced, and refactored—mirroring ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 practices but applied to learning outcomes, prerequisite graphs, and pedagogical mechanisms.

They integrate **Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)** and **Cognitive Task Analysis (CTA)** extensions into curriculum architecture, using intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load metrics to quantitatively evaluate prerequisite sequencing, abstraction layering, and worked-example fading strategies across multi-year computing programs.

Key next sources focus on:
- Explicit architectural knowledge management for curricula
- Pattern-oriented curriculum design at scale
- Empirical measurement of cognitive load in complex computing topics
- Formal curriculum refactoring and evolution models
