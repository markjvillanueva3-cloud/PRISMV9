## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Platform Business Model Innovation, Incentive Design, and Dynamic Pricing Mechanisms**

A world-leading expert next masters **multi-sided platform incentive alignment under asymmetric information**, **dynamic pricing with network effects and learning**, and **platform governance through contractual and architectural levers** drawn from advanced industrial organization and strategy literature.

Core uncovered areas include:
- Optimal subsidy and pricing trajectories in growing platforms with forward-looking users (dynamic two-sided pricing with installed-base effects).
- Mechanism design for developer/user participation when quality is unobservable (adverse selection and moral hazard in ecosystems).
- Choice of governance form—marketplace vs. hybrid vs. vertically integrated—based on spillovers and coordination costs.
- Ramsey-style pricing extended to multi-sided settings with congestion and matching frictions.

**Key next sources:**

- **Rochet & Tirole (2011)** “Platform Pricing” in *Handbook of Digital Economics* (extended Ramsey analysis with multidimensional externalities).
- **Hagiu & Wright (2020)** “When Do Platforms Create Value? A Theory of Multi-Sided Platforms” (incentive alignment and value creation under asymmetric information).
- **MIT OCW 15.358** Platform Strategy, Spring 2021 – Module on “Dynamic Pricing and Learning in Two-Sided Markets” (uses structural models from empirical IO).
- **Parker, Van Alstyne & Jiang (2021)** “Platform Governance: Designing for Innovation and Incentive Alignment” (Strategic Management Journal follow-on framework).
- **NIST SP 2200-6 (2024)** “Incentive Mechanisms and Contract Design in Software Platform Ecosystems” (focuses on observable vs. non-observable effort).
- **Weyl & Fabinger (2013)** “Pass-Through and Demand Rationing in Multi-Sided Markets” (American Economic Review theory extension).

These build directly on the listed two-sided market canon by shifting from static pricing to **dynamic mechanism design, governance architecture, and empirical structural estimation** of platform incentives.
