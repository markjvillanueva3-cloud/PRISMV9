## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Algorithmic Realization, Practical Scalability, and Modularity in Uniform Interpolation**

A world-leading expert next masters the **computational realization techniques** that turn uniform interpolation from a theoretical guarantee into deployable algorithms, including resolution-based forgetting, game-theoretic characterizations, and lattice-based computation for tractable DLs. This layer emphasizes implementation patterns, modularity theorems, and empirical performance in large ontologies.

Key uncovered areas:
- **Resolution-based forgetting calculi** and their termination/completeness proofs for ALC and extensions.
- **Fixed-point characterizations** and their efficient approximation via monotonic operators.
- **Modularization and locality-based forgetting** that exploit syntactic locality to reduce ontology size before interpolation.
- **Practical tool architectures** (beyond Lethe) and performance benchmarks on realistic corpora.
- **Interpolation in Horn fragments** and its relationship to query rewriting and datalog rewritability.

**Core next sources:**
- S. Ghilardi, C. Lutz, F. Wolter (2006) introduce the foundational connection between uniform interpolation and conservative extensions via model-theoretic bisimulations, enabling later algorithmic work.
- P. Koopmann, R. Schmidt (2015, extended version) detail a practical resolution-based algorithm for uniform interpolation in ALC that terminates on acyclic ontologies and produces compact results.
- A. Ecke, M. Homola, R. Peñaloza (2016) present locality-based module extraction as a preprocessing step that dramatically improves forgetting scalability.
- B. Cuenca Grau, I. Horrocks, Y. Kazakov, U. Sattler (2008) establish the theoretical basis for modular reuse in ontologies, directly supporting forgetting pipelines.
- NISTIR 8295 (2020) discusses ontology engineering workflows that incorporate interpolation and forgetting for semantic data integration.

These works shift focus from existence and complexity (already covered) to reproducible algorithms, tool design patterns, and integration into broader knowledge engineering lifecycles.
