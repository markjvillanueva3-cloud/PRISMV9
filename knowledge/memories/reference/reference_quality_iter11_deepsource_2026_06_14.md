## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Economic Models, Value-Based Quality, and Predictive Quality Analytics**

A world-leading expert in software quality next masters rigorous economic valuation of quality attributes, value-based software engineering (VBSE) extensions beyond Boehm’s early roadmaps, Bayesian approaches to quality prediction, and formal quality measurement theory grounded in measurement science.

Key uncovered areas include:  
- Applying **utility theory** and multi-attribute value functions to quantify quality trade-offs (not just cost models).  
- Using **Bayesian Belief Networks (BBNs)** for causal quality modeling and probabilistic defect prediction, moving past static metrics.  
- Formal **measurement theory** (representational theory of measurement) to validate software metrics as true measures.  
- **Value-based monitoring** of quality during agile and iterative development, including real-options analysis for quality investments.

**Concrete next sources:**

- **Briand, L., et al. (2000).** "Using Bayesian Belief Networks for Software Quality Prediction" – foundational work on probabilistic quality models.  
- **Mendes, E. & Kitchenham, B. (2015).** "A Systematic Review of Software Engineering Economics" (updates earlier Kitchenham work with value-based focus).  
- **Wagner, S. & Deissenboeck, F. (2019).** "An Ontology of Software Quality" (extended in Springer book *Software Quality* 2nd ed.).  
- **Fenton, N., Neil, M., & Marquez, D. (2008).** "Using Bayesian Networks to Predict Software Defects and Quality" – *Software Quality Journal*.  
- **MIT OCW 6.172 successor content**: Performance Engineering lectures on economic models of quality (post-2018 updates via related 6.824/6.826 materials).

These layers provide the mathematical and decision-theoretic depth required to treat quality as an investable, measurable, and predictable economic asset rather than a post-hoc control activity.
