## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Cognitive and Perceptual Foundations for System Visualization**

A world-leading system-viz expert must master the rigorous perceptual and cognitive science that underpins effective visual notations for software architecture, dynamic system behavior, and runtime structure. This layer focuses on formal models of visual perception, pre-attentive processing, Gestalt principles applied to diagrams, and empirical validation methods for software visualizations—grounded in cognitive psychology and human-computer interaction literature rather than general design.

Key areas include:
- **Pre-attentive visual processing** and its direct application to notation design (color, form, motion, texture pop-out).
- **Gestalt laws** formalized for graph and architecture diagrams (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, connectedness).
- **Cognitive load theory** (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) in the context of complex software visualizations.
- **Visual working memory** limits and chunking strategies for multi-view system dashboards.
- **Empirical evaluation frameworks** beyond cognitive dimensions, including controlled experiments on comprehension accuracy and speed for software architecture diagrams.

**Next authoritative sources to master:**

- **Cleveland WS (1993)** – Visualizing Data – foundational perceptual metrics for quantitative graphical perception.
- **Healey CG, Enns JT (2012)** – Attention and Visual Memory in Visualization and Computer Graphics – IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics.
- **Munzner T (2014)** – Visualization Analysis and Design – AK Peters / CRC Press (especially Chapters 4–7 on perception, color, and task abstraction).
- **Tufte ER (2001)** – The Visual Display of Quantitative Information, 2nd ed. – Graphics Press (perceptual psychology sections).
- **Larkin JH, Simon HA (1987)** – Why a Diagram is (Sometimes) Worth Ten Thousand Words – Cognitive Science.
- **Scaife M, Rogers Y (1996)** – External Cognition: How Do Graphical Representations Work? – International Journal of Human-Computer Studies.

These provide the institutional and theoretical depth required for designing and evaluating system-viz notations at PRISM scale.
