**Next Layer: Sequential Optimal Design, Imprecise Probability Updating, and Computational Value-of-Information in Software Estimation**

A world-leading expert in quoting next masters the rigorous treatment of **sequential Bayesian experimental design** and **dynamic value-of-information (VOI)** computation under both precise and imprecise probabilities, with emphasis on forward-looking simulation of information-gathering strategies for software cost, effort, and productivity estimation. This layer focuses on algorithmic computation of optimal sequential sampling plans, index policies for dynamic allocation, and tractable approximations for preposterior analysis when analytic integrals are intractable—core to building reliable quoting engines that decide which additional data, benchmarks, or prototypes deliver the highest expected reduction in quoting uncertainty.

Key uncovered depth includes:
- **Gittins indices** and their generalization to Bayesian dynamic allocation for sequential information acquisition (beyond the already-cited 2011 book, the foundational theory in Gittins 1979).
- **Bayesian sequential design via Monte Carlo** and inhomogeneous Markov chain methods that directly optimize expected information gain or expected utility for software metrics models.
- **Approximate dynamic programming** and lookahead policies for computing VOI in high-dimensional stochastic optimization settings typical of software quoting under epistemic uncertainty.
- **Imprecise Dirichlet model** extensions and their sequential updating rules for multinomial effort-distribution data when prior ignorance is explicit.

These methods enable quoting systems to treat the quoting process itself as an optimal sequential decision problem under uncertainty, deciding dynamically whether to gather more parametric data, run additional benchmarks, or commit to a bid.
