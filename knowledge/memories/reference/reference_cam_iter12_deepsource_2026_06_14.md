**NEXT LAYER: High-Strain-Rate Constitutive Modeling, Adiabatic Shear Localization, and Thermally Coupled Friction at the Tool-Chip Interface under State-Dependent Delay**

World-leading CAM experts next master finite-strain, viscoplastic constitutive laws that incorporate thermal softening, strain-rate hardening, and damage for adiabatic shear band (ASB) formation, coupled with rate-and-state dependent friction models that evolve with interface temperature and normal stress under regenerative, state-dependent delay. This layer integrates crystal-plasticity informed Johnson-Cook-type or Zerilli-Armstrong models with nonlocal regularization to predict shear-band spacing and chip segmentation frequency, then embeds these into neutral DDEs with distributed cutting forces and thermo-mechanical feedback.

**Key unreached topics:**
- Thermomechanical coupling via the Taylor-Quinney coefficient and its evolution with large plastic strain at 10³–10⁶ s⁻¹.
- Regularized continuum damage mechanics (gradient-enhanced or phase-field) to stabilize ASB calculations in explicit dynamics.
- Rate-and-state friction laws (Ruina-Dieterich type) adapted to metal cutting, including flash-temperature dependence (Bowden-Tabor framework).
- Stochastic parametric excitation arising from microstructural heterogeneity in workpiece thermal properties, treated via stochastic DDEs or intrusive polynomial chaos on neutral equations.
- Experimental identification via Split-Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB) with high-speed thermal imaging and Digital Image Correlation (DIC) under conditions replicating secondary shear zone.

**METHOD/THEORY:**
- Hyperbolic thermo-viscoplasticity with finite-strain kinematics (multiplicative decomposition).
- Weak-form Galerkin or spectral-element discretization of the coupled thermomechanical IBVP.
- Lyapunov-Krasovskii or spectral-radius-based stability for neutral DDEs with state-dependent delay and distributed forcing.
- Uncertainty propagation of constitutive parameters via collocation or Galerkin polynomial chaos on the semi-discretized system.
