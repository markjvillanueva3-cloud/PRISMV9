This layer foregrounds how sociotechnical systems actively perform strategic forgetting through architectural decisions, classification schemas, and boundary-object plasticity.

Key next sources include:

- **Bowker & Star (1999)** on how classification infrastructures systematically “torque” and erase local knowledge while appearing neutral.

- **Star (2010)** posthumous synthesis on boundary objects and the “ecology of infrastructure” with explicit focus on methodological inversion techniques for software-intensive settings.

- **Ribes & Finholt (2009)** on the long now of infrastructure: temporal scaling, path dependency, and institutional forgetting in large-scale scientific software platforms.
