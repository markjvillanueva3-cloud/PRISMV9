## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Institutional Logics and the Multiplicity of Infrastructural Orders**

A world-leading expert in PRISM’s tribal-knowledge galaxy next masters how multiple, often contradictory *institutional logics* interact within the classification systems, data models, and knowledge infrastructures that manufacturing intelligence platforms must stabilize. This layer moves beyond single-institution or boundary-object accounts to examine how competing logics (professional, corporate, market, community, state) are embedded in, maintained, or transformed through the design and evolution of information infrastructures.

Core depth includes:
- Friedland & Alford’s (1991) foundational theory of institutional logics as the axial principles that organize societal domains and generate organizational identities.
- Thornton, Ocasio & Lounsbury’s (2012) elaboration of the institutional logics perspective, including the concept of *institutional complexity* and *hybrid organizations*—directly applicable to platforms that must reconcile engineering, quality, regulatory, and commercial logics.
- The methodological turn to *institutional work* that enacts, maintains, or disrupts these logics inside infrastructural projects (Lawrence & Suddaby, 2006 already covered; extended by Lawrence, Leca & Zilber, 2013).
- Empirical infrastructure studies that trace *logics-in-practice* in large-scale information systems (e.g., how ERP/PLM/MES platforms become sites of institutional struggle).

This layer supplies the analytic tools to explain why certain classificatory schemes or data standards persist or fracture inside manufacturing intelligence platforms, and how architects can intentionally perform institutional work to stabilize or evolve the platform’s epistemic order.
