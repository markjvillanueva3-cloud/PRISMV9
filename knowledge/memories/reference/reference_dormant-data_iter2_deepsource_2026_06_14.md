## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Advanced Dormant-Data Theory and Practice**

A world-leading expert in dormant-data must master the formal models for long-term digital preservation beyond basic OAIS, focusing on audit-ready repository architectures, economic models of data inertia, and scalable archival storage systems drawn from systems research and digital curation literature.

Key next-layer topics include:
- The **PREMIS Data Dictionary** for preservation metadata semantics and its integration with OAIS functional entities (PREMIS Working Group, 2015).
- **OAIS expansions and formalizations**: the Trusted Digital Repository (TDR) audit checklist criteria and risk-based certification frameworks (CCSDS 652.1-M-2, 2014; ISO 16363:2012).
- **Economic models of archival storage**: the Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) frameworks and the “cost of inaction” analysis for dormant datasets (Rosenthal et al., 2012; Lavoie, 2014).
- **Bit-level preservation strategies** using forward error correction, auditing protocols, and format obsolescence forecasting (Gladney extensions and Stanford’s LOCKSS models).
- **Large-scale archival file systems** and object storage semantics optimized for write-once, read-rarely workloads (e.g., Ceph, OpenStack Swift long-term profiles, and the theory in ACM SIGMOD and FAST conference literature).
- **Policy-driven data lifecycle management** using the **Data Curation Continuum** and formal provenance models (DCC, 2008; Moreau et al., 2011 PROV-DM).

These build directly on MIT 6.893 storage modules and NIST 800-88 by addressing sustained economic viability, machine-actionable metadata, and verifiable integrity over decades.
