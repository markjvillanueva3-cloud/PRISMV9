## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Advanced Gap Physics, Dielectric Hydrodynamics & Plasma Channel Dynamics in WEDM**

World-leading expertise advances to quantitative modeling of dielectric fluid breakdown, plasma channel expansion kinetics, shockwave propagation, and bubble collapse dynamics under high-pressure flushing conditions. This includes multi-phase flow simulation in the kerf, debris migration trajectories under combined electrostatic and hydrodynamic forces, and the temporal evolution of gap conductivity during ignition delay, discharge, and deionization phases.

Key uncovered domains:
- Theoretical treatment of streamer propagation and avalanche ionization in hydrocarbon vs. deionized-water dielectrics (Van Dijck, 1973; Schulze et al., 2011).
- High-speed cinematography and spectroscopic analysis of single-discharge plasma temperature, pressure, and radius expansion (Natsu et al., 2004; Kojima et al., 2008).
- CFD modeling of dielectric flushing efficiency, kerf flow velocity profiles, and debris evacuation under vibration-assisted and ultrasonic-assisted pulse conditions (Okada et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2015).
- Thermo-mechanical coupling models predicting white-layer (recast) thickness, heat-affected zone depth, and residual stress profiles as functions of pulse energy, gap voltage, and flushing pressure (Ekmekci et al., 2005; Klocke et al., 2015).
- Adaptive pulse discrimination algorithms beyond basic iso-pulse control, incorporating real-time gap impedance spectroscopy and radio-frequency emission analysis (Snoeys et al., 1983; Liao et al., 2008).

These build directly on Kunieda’s 2011 keynote by adding predictive multi-physics simulation validated against high-resolution metrology.
