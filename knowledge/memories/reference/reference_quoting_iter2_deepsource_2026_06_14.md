**Next Layer: Parametric Modeling, Algorithmic Costing, and Uncertainty Quantification in Software Quoting**

A world-leading expert in software quoting next masters formal parametric cost modeling theory, statistical calibration techniques, Bayesian inference for estimation, and quantitative risk aggregation methods drawn from software engineering economics and empirical SE research.

Key areas include:
- COCOMO II model calibration, local calibration using Bayesian methods, and the role of scale factors and effort multipliers in algorithmic estimation.
- Constructive Cost Model extensions to systems engineering (COSYSMO) parameter tuning and productivity drivers.
- Quantitative risk analysis using Monte Carlo simulation, analytic hierarchy process (AHP) for cost driver weighting, and probabilistic sensitivity analysis.
- Empirical benchmarking databases and statistical techniques for effort prediction model validation (MMRE, PRED, balanced mean magnitude of relative error).
- Theory of estimation as a decision-making process under uncertainty, including reference class forecasting and debiasing techniques.

These build directly on foundational Boehm and Valerdi works by adding rigorous statistical and Bayesian layers for higher-fidelity quoting in complex software-intensive projects.
