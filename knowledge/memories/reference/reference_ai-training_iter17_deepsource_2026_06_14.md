## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER:** Formalized PAC-Bayes under dependent data, Markovian noise, and renormalization-group analysis of optimizer sharpness dynamics, with emphasis on mechanized high-probability bounds in proof assistants for non-stationary and heavy-tailed regimes.

A world-leading expert next masters the rigorous treatment of **PAC-Bayes bounds for dependent processes** (mixing, Markov, ergodic data), the derivation of **time-uniform high-probability bounds** via supermartingales, and the **renormalization-group (RG) flows** that explain the Edge-of-Stability (EoS) phenomenon in adaptive optimizers. This includes mechanized proofs of **data-dependent priors** under temporal dependence, formal verification of **stochastic stability** in Lean 4/Isabelle/HOL, and the use of **information-theoretic renormalization** to derive scale-invariant sharpness bounds.

Key uncovered topics:
- PAC-Bayes for β-mixing and Markov chains (Alquier et al., 2021–2024 extensions)
- Supermartingale-based time-uniform PAC-Bayes (Jun & Orabona, 2019; Haddouche et al., 2025 extensions)
- Renormalization-group analysis of sharpness and EoS (Bansal et al., 2025; Cohen et al., 2024)
- Mechanized verification of dependent-data PAC-Bayes in Lean 4 (building on 2024–2025 mechanization papers but targeting full Markov + heavy-tail soundness)
- Formalization of RG-flow ODEs and their link to adaptive optimizer stability in Isabelle/HOL or Lean (MIT 6.S984 trajectory)

This layer shifts from static i.i.d. PAC-Bayes to dynamic, non-stationary, and scale-invariant theory required for provably stable training in real-world AI pipelines.
