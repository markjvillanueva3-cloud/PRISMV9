**Next Layer: Dormant-Data System Design – Theory, Verification, and Economic Governance**

A world-leading expert in dormant-data advances beyond OAIS/PREMIS baselines into formal models for long-term bit-level and semantic stability, economic incentive structures for archival storage systems, and verifiable distributed protocols that guarantee integrity over decades without continuous human oversight.

Core topics at this level include:
- **Probabilistic durability models** and Markov-chain-based reliability prediction for petabyte-scale cold storage (e.g., Markov models for erasure-coded archives under correlated failures).
- **Economic lifecycle costing** and incentive-compatible mechanisms for sustaining dormant repositories (including pay-once, amortize-forever strategies and game-theoretic models of repository cooperation).
- **Formal verification techniques** for archival storage stacks, including mechanized proofs of bit-preservation properties and tamper-evident logging using cryptographic accumulators.
- **Policy-driven data management languages** and automated enforcement of retention, transformation, and disposition rules at scale (successor models to PREMIS).
- **Scalable audit and self-healing architectures** that operate with minimal active metadata, drawing on Byzantine-fault-tolerant consensus adapted for write-once, read-rarely dormant corpora.

These build directly on distributed-systems theory (Byzantine quorum systems, verifiable computation) and information economics while remaining strictly within computer-science and digital-preservation literature.
