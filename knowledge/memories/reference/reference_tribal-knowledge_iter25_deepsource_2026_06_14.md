## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
The next layer in tribal-knowledge for software-engineering organizations centers on absorptive capacity for assimilating external technical insights, the SECI spiral for converting developer tacit expertise into codified team assets, Schein's multi-level culture model explaining persistent "how we do things here" norms in codebases and DevOps groups, and standardized KM system requirements for sustaining epistemic flows across distributed engineering boundaries. These extend prior situated-learning and sensemaking foundations by providing measurable mechanisms (e.g., prior related knowledge as predictor of innovation uptake) and institutional processes tailored to agile and globally-distributed software teams.

Depth draws from organizational theory applied to CS contexts: Cohen & Levinthal quantify R&D "learning curves" that determine whether tribal knowledge accumulates or atrophies; Nonaka & Takeuchi supply the socialization-externalization-combination-internalization cycle used in engineering knowledge-creation platforms; Schein dissects visible artifacts (tools, rituals) versus invisible assumptions that either amplify or block knowledge sharing in high-talent software organizations; ISO 30401 supplies auditable clauses on knowledge mapping, competence networks, and continual improvement applicable to CMMI-aligned engineering processes.
