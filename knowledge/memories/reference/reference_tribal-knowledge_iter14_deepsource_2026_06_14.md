## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Institutional forgetting, infrastructural inversion, and the abductive construction of "tribal knowledge" as infrastructural memory work in software-engineering organizations.**

A world-leading expert next masters the mechanisms by which organizations systematically *forget* competing institutional logics while infrastructurally embedding selective remembering into sociomaterial routines. This layer foregrounds how tribal knowledge is not merely transmitted but actively produced through infrastructural inversion, boundary-object plasticity, and abductive theorizing under institutional complexity.

Key uncovered domains include:
- **Organizational forgetting as deliberate institutional work** (beyond de Holan & Phillips 2004)
- **Infrastructural inversion and invisible work** as methods for surfacing embedded tribal knowledge
- **Abductive analysis** as the rigorous methodological bridge between grounded theory and institutional theory in CS-adjacent qualitative research
- **Rhetorical history and collective memory** as active engineering of software-team identity and standards

**Core next sources:**
- Bowker (1994) on infrastructural inversion as the deliberate foregrounding of normally invisible infrastructural substrates that carry organizational memory.
- Star & Bowker (2007) on how classification systems and standards become "invisible allies" that perform forgetting.
- Timmermans & Tavory (2012) already listed; the expert advances to their later refinement of abductive analysis techniques tailored to sociotechnical settings.
- Hatch & Schultz (2017) on organizational identity and the strategic use of forgetting in rhetorical history work.
- Carlile (2004) on pragmatic boundaries and the transformation of knowledge across syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic boundaries in product development (critical for software-engineering tribal knowledge).

These provide the institutional, methodological, and infrastructural theory depth required to treat tribal knowledge as engineered sociomaterial infrastructure rather than tacit residue.
