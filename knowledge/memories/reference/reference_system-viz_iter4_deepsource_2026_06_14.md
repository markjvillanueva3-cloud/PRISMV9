## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Perceptual and Cognitive Foundations for System Visualization at Scale**

A world-leading system-viz expert next masters **perceptual psychology and cognitive modeling** applied specifically to software architecture and dynamic system diagrams, moving beyond general perception to rigorous models of how engineers encode, decode, and update mental models of large-scale software systems under real-time constraints.

Core topics include:
- **Pre-attentive visual channels** and their ranked effectiveness for encoding software metrics in polymetric and architecture views.
- **Cognitive load theory** (Sweller) and **dual-coding theory** (Paivio) applied to diagrammatic reasoning in software engineering.
- **Visual working memory limitations** (Luck & Vogel) and change blindness in live system visualizations.
- **Graph comprehension models** (Pinker's theory of graph comprehension, 1990; Carpenter & Shah, 1998).
- **Constraint-based layout aesthetics** and their measurable impact on comprehension time and accuracy in software dependency graphs.
- **Notational semiotics** and **visual syntax theory** (beyond Moody’s Physics of Notations) drawing from formal diagrammatic reasoning literature.
- **Animation and transition design** for dynamic architecture views (Heer & Robertson, 2007).

These layers are drawn from advanced visual cognition courses, specialized textbooks on diagram perception, and empirical software visualization experiments.
