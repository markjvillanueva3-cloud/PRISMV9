## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: High-Strain-Rate Constitutive Modeling, Friction at Tool-Chip Interface under Extreme Conditions, and Coupled Thermo-Mechanical FEM Validation Protocols**

World-leading mastery next requires rigorous treatment of viscoplastic constitutive laws valid at 10³–10⁶ s⁻¹, adiabatic shear banding criteria, and temperature/strain-rate dependent friction models that replace merchant/oxley assumptions. This includes split-Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) derived Johnson-Cook, Zerilli-Armstrong, and Steinberg-Guinan models calibrated for machining-relevant strain rates and temperatures, plus their implementation in explicit FEM with element deletion and remeshing strategies.

Key theories:  
- High-strain-rate plasticity and dynamic recovery/recrystallization in ferritic, austenitic, and titanium alloys.  
- Sticking-sliding friction transitions and velocity-dependent friction laws (e.g., Usui, Iwata, Childs-type models).  
- Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) and arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) formulations for chip formation without predefined separation lines.  
- Validation against high-speed camera chip morphology, infrared thermography, and force dynamometry uncertainty budgets per NIST protocols.

**METHOD/THEORY/standards depth:**  
- Constitutive model calibration from SHPB + orthogonal cutting inverse methods (Jaspers-type).  
- Friction characterization via split-tool and quick-stop tests at elevated temperatures.  
- ASME B5.54 and ISO 230-3 uncertainty propagation into thermal error and force signals.  
- Mesh convergence, artificial damping, and hourglass control in explicit codes for adiabatic shear localization.
