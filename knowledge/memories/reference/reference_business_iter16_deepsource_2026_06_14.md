## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Platform Information Design, Algorithmic Gatekeeping, and Dynamic Remedy Mechanisms in Regulated Digital Ecosystems**

A world-leading expert next masters the micro-foundations of **information design under strategic platform intermediation**, focusing on how platforms optimally design information flows, ranking algorithms, and disclosure rules while internalizing regulatory constraints from DMA-style gatekeeper obligations and Section 5 unfairness standards. This layer emphasizes mechanism-design approaches to platform governance that go beyond static transparency standards (IEEE 7012) into dynamic, multi-period information architectures that deter tacit algorithmic collusion and self-preferencing without destroying innovation incentives.

Core uncovered knowledge includes:

- **Multi-sided information design with endogenous feedback loops**: how platforms choose what signals to reveal to different sides (users, complementors, advertisers) when those signals affect both demand and competitive entry (Bergemann, Brooks & Morris extensions into dynamic settings).
- **Regulatory mechanism design for gatekeeper compliance**: optimal auditing and remedy structures that satisfy ex-ante DMA fair-ranking and data-access obligations while minimizing rent extraction.
- **Collusion-proof information structures in algorithmic ecosystems**: Bayesian persuasion techniques adapted to repeated oligopoly with learning algorithms (building on but extending Acemoglu et al. 2023 and NIST SP 2200-11).

**Key next sources** (college-level depth):

- MIT OCW 14.03 Microeconomic Theory and Public Policy, Fall 2022 – Module on “Information Design and Platforms” (Tirole-influenced approach).
- Bergemann, D. & Bonatti, A. (2023). *The Economics of Information Design* (Princeton University Press, forthcoming chapters on platforms).
- FTC (2023). *Policy Statement on Algorithmic Pricing and Collusion* (internal staff working paper series).
- IEEE P2981 (draft 2024). *Standard for the Design of Transparent and Explainable AI Systems in Platform Contexts*.

These provide the rigorous contract-theoretic and market-design toolkit required for PRISM’s business galaxy to model platform strategy under evolving antitrust and transparency regimes.
