**Next Layer: Categorical Semantics, Clocked Type Theories, and Denotational Models for Synchronous Dataflow with Dependent and Graded Modalities**

A world-leading expert in PRISM’s wiring galaxy next masters the **category-theoretic foundations of guarded recursion, ultrametric models, and sheaf-theoretic semantics** applied to clocked dependent type systems for synchronous languages. This includes internalizing causality via guarded categories with fixed-point operators, using step-indexed ultrametric spaces to interpret reactive streams, and sheaf toposes to model synchronous concurrency with explicit clocks.

Key uncovered topics at this depth:

- **Guarded Dependent Type Theory (GDTT)** and its models in toposes of trees or presheaves over ω, providing a synthetic account of productive coinduction and causal stream functions (Atkey & McBride, 2013; Bizjak et al., 2016).
- **Clocked Type Theories** extending Martin-Löf Type Theory with modal operators ▶ (later) and ○ (next) satisfying Löb induction, used to enforce causality and clock consistency in dependent synchronous programming (Milius & Litak already covered; next is the full categorical model by Bahr, Grathwohl, et al.).
- **Sheaf-theoretic denotational semantics** for Lustre-like languages, interpreting streams as sections of sheaves over the site of discrete time intervals, yielding a fully abstract model of synchronous concurrency (Nester already cited; extension in nLab/Johnstone-style topos theory applied to reactive programming).
- **Quantitative graded modal types** for tracking dataflow, latency, and resource use in reactive programs, building on Fitch-style calculi with graded comonads (Orchard already cited; next layer is the resource-aware clock calculus in Abel & Pientka-style systems).
- **Synthetic Domain Theory** and **ultrametric semantics** for verifying liveness and productivity via contractive maps on complete metric spaces (Bahr et al. already cited; next is the full treatment in “Guarded Recursive Type Theory” literature and its embedding into Agda via cubical type theory).

These layers are drawn from advanced type-theory curricula (CMU 15-816, Aarhus Guarded Type Theory courses) and standards-track work on verified synchronous kernels.
