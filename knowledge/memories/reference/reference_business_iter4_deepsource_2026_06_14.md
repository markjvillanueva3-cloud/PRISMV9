## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Platform Pricing, Monetization Architecture, and Value-Capture Theory**

A world-leading expert next masters advanced multi-sided pricing mechanisms, value-capture strategies under network effects, and formal models of platform revenue architecture. This layer focuses on optimal price structure design (which side to subsidize, when to use freemium, usage-based vs. subscription vs. transaction fees), dynamic pricing in winner-take-most markets, and integration of data-driven monetization with governance.

Key uncovered areas include:
- Rochet-Tirole price theory extensions and welfare implications
- Formal models of freemium conversion funnels and tiered pricing in B2B platforms
- Metered vs. non-metered pricing in SaaS/manufacturing-intelligence platforms
- Antitrust implications of zero-price strategies and cross-subsidization

**Concrete next sources:**

- **Rochet & Tirole (2003)** “Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets” (Journal of the European Economic Association, 1(4)) – foundational pricing model.
- **Armstrong (2006)** “Competition in Two-Sided Markets” (RAND Journal of Economics, 37(3)) – price discrimination and monopoly vs. duopoly analysis.
- **Weyl (2010)** “A Price Theory of Multi-Sided Platforms” (American Economic Review, 100(4)) – marginal-cost pricing rules for platforms.
- **Eisenmann, Parker & Van Alstyne (2011)** “Platform Envelopment” extensions in follow-on pricing literature.
- **Parker, Van Alstyne & Jiang (2017)** “Platform Ecosystems: How Developers Invent the Future” (chapter on monetization architecture).
- **MIT OCW 15.351** “Introduction to Innovation and Entrepreneurship” (Spring 2022) – pricing module for platform ventures.
- **BLS Working Paper WP-533 (2021)** “Pricing Strategies of Digital Platforms” (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics).
- **NIST SP 2200-3 (2020)** “Economic Analysis of Software Pricing and Bundling Models” (National Institute of Standards and Technology).

These provide rigorous mathematical frameworks and empirical methods for designing PRISM’s pricing architecture as a manufacturing-intelligence platform.
