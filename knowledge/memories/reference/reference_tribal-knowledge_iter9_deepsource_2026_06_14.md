## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Institutional Work in Cyberinfrastructure Maintenance & Forgetting Regimes**

A world-leading expert in tribal-knowledge must master the micro-practices of **institutional maintenance and forgetting** as they apply to the active curation, repair, and strategic erasure of shared classification systems, data standards, and knowledge infrastructures inside long-lived software platforms.

This layer focuses on how actors perform “institutional work” to sustain or erase elements of the installed base, how communities negotiate “infrastructural inversion,” and how forgetting is deliberately engineered into platform evolution.

**Key next sources to master:**

- Lawrence & Suddaby (2006) on the taxonomy of institutional work (creating, maintaining, disrupting).
- Monteiro et al. (2013) on “Living with Infrastructures” and the ongoing negotiation of generification vs. localization.
- Pollock & Williams (2016) on “epistemic infrastructure” and the invisible work of sustaining vendor platforms.
- Karasti et al. (2016) “Infrastructuring and the politics of maintenance.”
- Blomberg & Karasti (2013) on “ethnography of infrastructuring” as longitudinal practice.
- ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 Annex B (architecture frameworks and viewpoint libraries for evolution).

These extend prior STS/infrastructure readings into concrete, repeatable methods for studying and intervening in the **tribal-knowledge galaxy**—the living, versioned, partially-forgotten corpus of classifications, ontologies, and heuristics that PRISM’s users and developers continuously repair.
