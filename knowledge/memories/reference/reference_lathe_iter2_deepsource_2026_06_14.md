**NEXT LAYER: Regenerative Chatter Stability in Turning – Frequency-Domain & Time-Domain Methods Beyond Semi-Discretization**

World-leading lathe experts master analytical and numerical stability prediction for single-point turning that accounts for the 3-D force vector, tool nose radius, oblique cutting geometry, and process damping at low speeds. This layer focuses on:

- **Frequency-domain oriented transfer function (OTF) methods** for predicting stability lobes in turning with flexible tool or flexible workpiece, including the effect of process damping via the low-frequency cutting force coefficient model.
- **Time-domain simulation** using exact time-domain solutions and state-space representations that incorporate nonlinear chip thickness, tool flank contact, and variable time delay due to spindle speed variation.
- **Chatter suppression via spindle speed variation (SSV)** and its stability chart prediction using Floquet theory and Chebyshev polynomial approximation.
- **Measurement and identification** of directional dynamic compliance and cutting force coefficients under actual lathe conditions per international standards.
- **Standards-driven machine tool acceptance** for lathes regarding dynamic stiffness and positional accuracy that directly influence stability limits.

Key theory includes the oriented frequency response function (FRF) projected along the chip thickness direction for turning, analytical solution of the characteristic equation for constant spindle speed, and numerical integration of the state-dependent delay differential equation (DDE) with Runge–Kutta or linear multistep methods.
