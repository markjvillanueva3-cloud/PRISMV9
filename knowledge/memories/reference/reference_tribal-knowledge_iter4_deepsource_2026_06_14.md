## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Epistemic and Socio-Technical Foundations of Knowledge Ecosystems in Software Organizations**

A world-leading expert in tribal-knowledge next masters the micro-foundations of knowledge integration and epistemic coordination in large-scale software systems. This layer moves beyond SECI, Ba, and high-level KM systems to examine how distributed software teams create, negotiate, and stabilize epistemic artifacts across boundaries, drawing on organizational epistemology, boundary objects, and socio-technical systems theory.

Key next-tier knowledge includes:

- **Boundary Objects and Infrastructural Inversion** (Star & Griesemer 1989; Bowker & Star 1999) – how classification systems, APIs, data schemas, and architectural decision records function as plastic yet robust artifacts that coordinate tacit knowledge across communities of practice in software engineering.

- **Epistemic Cultures and Knowledge-Making Practices** (Knorr Cetina 1999) – comparative analysis of how high-energy physics, molecular biology, and software engineering construct “truth” differently; applied to why software’s epistemic culture favors working code, continuous integration, and living documentation over formal proof.

- **Knowledge Integration in Product Development** (Grant 1996; Carlile 2002, 2004) – pragmatic, semantic, and syntactic boundaries in software teams; the progression from transfer to translation to transformation of knowledge during agile scaling and platform engineering.

- **Transactive Memory Systems (TMS) in Software Teams** (Wegner 1987; Faraj & Sproull 2000; Ren & Argote 2011) – how expert directories, code ownership graphs, and tribal knowledge repositories encode “who knows what” in rapidly evolving codebases.

- **Organizational Epistemology and Absorptive Capacity in Software** (Cohen & Levinthal 1990; Lane et al. 2006; Tortoriello et al. 2012) – refined models of recognition, assimilation, and exploitation of external technical knowledge in open-source and inner-source contexts.

This layer is typically found in advanced graduate seminars on socio-technical systems, knowledge-intensive firms, and information systems research.
