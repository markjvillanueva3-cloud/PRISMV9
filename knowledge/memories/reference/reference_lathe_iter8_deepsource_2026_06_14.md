**Next Layer: Multi-Physics Regenerative Instability with In-Process Metrology and Closed-Loop Stability Control**

World-leading expertise advances to integrated thermo-mechanical-regenerative models that incorporate real-time in-process metrology for adaptive stability control, moving beyond offline simulation to closed-loop prediction and suppression under varying compliance and thermal boundary conditions.

Key topics include:
- Frequency-domain and time-domain stability prediction using measured dynamic compliance updated via spindle-mounted or tool-tip laser/vision metrology (NIST).
- State-dependent delay models augmented with thermomechanical flank contact and ploughing forces under varying nose radius and approach angle.
- In-process chatter detection and suppression via active/adaptive control using force, vibration, and acoustic emission fusion with Kalman-filter or observer-based estimation.
- Uncertainty quantification frameworks that propagate both aleatoric (material, geometry) and epistemic (model form) uncertainties through semi-discretization or Chebyshev collocation into probabilistic stability lobes.
- Calibration and verification protocols for turning stability under ASME B5.64 using non-contact dynamic spindle metrology and on-machine modal testing.
- Nonlinear regenerative effects including multiple regenerative waves, process damping variation with wear, and thermal softening feedback in hardened alloys.

This layer is drawn from advanced graduate coursework on manufacturing process control, standards for performance evaluation of CNC turning centers with stability annexes, and government metrology research focused on closed-loop manufacturing systems.
