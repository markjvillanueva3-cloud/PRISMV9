**Next Layer: Advanced Quoting Theory & Methodology**

A world-leading expert in software quoting next masters formal measurement theory applied to parametric models, value-driven decision analysis under deep uncertainty, and information quality frameworks for model-based cost estimation. This includes scale types and representational measurement theory for software metrics, multi-attribute value theory (MAVT) and multi-attribute utility theory (MAUT) for trade-off quantification in quoting, global sensitivity analysis for model robustness, and Bayesian model averaging for reconciling diverse software cost models.

Key uncovered areas include:

- **Representational Measurement Theory** and meaningfulness criteria when constructing quoting metrics (Krantz et al., 1971; Luce et al., 1990).
- **Value-Focused Thinking (VFT)** and decision conferencing techniques for eliciting stakeholder value models in software quoting (Keeney, 1992 – beyond the already-covered 1993 text).
- **Bayesian Model Averaging (BMA)** and Bayesian non-parametric methods for software cost model ensembles (Hoeting et al., 1999; Clyde & George, 2004).
- **Global Sensitivity Analysis (GSA)** using Sobol’ indices and variance-based decomposition specifically for parametric estimating models (Saltelli et al., 2010 – extension of the 2008 primer).
- **Information Quality (InfoQ)** framework assessing utility, analysis, and data quality dimensions for quoting information products (Kenett & Shmueli, 2016 – applied chapters on analytics quality not previously cited).
- **ISO/IEC 25012:2008** and **ISO/IEC 25040:2011** for data and evaluation quality models integrated into quoting processes.

These layers provide rigorous theoretical grounding for constructing defensible, auditable quoting systems in software engineering organizations.
