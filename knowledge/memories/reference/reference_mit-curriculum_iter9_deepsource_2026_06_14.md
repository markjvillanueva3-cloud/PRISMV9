## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Hierarchical Bayesian Models, Causal Intervention Frameworks, and Structured Skill Graph Inference for Adaptive Curriculum Sequencing**

A world-leading expert in mit-curriculum next masters the integration of hierarchical Bayesian cognitive modeling with causal discovery and graph neural inference to build verifiable, policy-optimizing curriculum engines. This layer moves beyond flat knowledge tracing into multi-level skill hierarchies, causal identifiability of learning interventions, and relational structure learning that respects prerequisite DAGs and transfer.

Key uncovered competencies:

- **Hierarchical Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (HBKT)** and empirical Bayes priors for individual differences (Cen et al. already covered; next is full variational inference over student-specific parameters and population hyperpriors).
- **Causal inference in student modeling**: do-calculus application to learning trajectories, counterfactual fairness in adaptive sequencing, and identifiability of teaching policies (extending Lee et al. 2021).
- **Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (RGCN)** and Graph Attention Networks (GAT) for automatic discovery of latent skill graphs from interaction data, including temporal and multimodal edges (building on Pardos et al. 2023).
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs)** with factored belief states over hierarchical skills, solved via Monte Carlo Tree Search or SARSOP for optimal curriculum planning (extending Rafferty 2016 and Lindner 2022).
- **Learning Progressions formalized as Bayesian Networks** with ordered multiple thresholds (OMT) and structured multidimensional item response theory (MIRT) aligned to IEEE competency definitions.
- **Computational modeling of conceptual change** using hierarchical Dirichlet processes and nonparametric Bayesian models of prerequisite discovery.

These draw directly from MIT’s graduate curriculum in computational cognitive science, probabilistic AI, and intelligent tutoring systems theory.
