**Next Layer: Advanced Perceptual Encoding, Architectural Viewpoint Semantics, and Empirical Evaluation Frameworks for System Visualization**

A world-leading expert in system-viz next masters the integration of **pre-attentive visual channels with hierarchical software architecture semantics**, formal viewpoint ontologies, and rigorous empirical methods for evaluating visual notations in large-scale system comprehension.

Key uncovered areas include:

- **Munzner’s nested model** for visualization design and validation, emphasizing abstraction, idiom, and algorithm layers tied to domain-specific data semantics (Munzner, 2009; 2014).
- **Green and Petre’s** extension of Cognitive Dimensions of Notations with empirical metrics for viscosity, hidden dependencies, and premature commitment in diagrammatic system representations (Green, 1996; Green & Petre, 1996).
- **IEEE 42010 (ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2011)** standardized ontology for architecture description, viewpoints, and visual models, including formal correspondence rules between views (ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010, 2011).
- **Larkin and Simon’s** cognitive theory of diagrammatic reasoning, focusing on computational efficiency of location-based vs. symbolic encodings in system diagrams (Larkin & Simon, 1987).
- **Bostock et al.’s** declarative grammar of graphics and perceptual benchmarking methods later formalized in D3 and Vega, providing measurable channels for encoding software metrics (Bostock et al., 2011; Heer & Bostock, 2010).
- Advanced **visual working memory load** models applied to multi-view system dashboards, building on Cowan’s embedded-processes framework and scene perception research (Cowan, 2001; Oliva, 2005 – MIT 9.459 Scene Representation and Perception, Spring 2009).

These layers add institutional depth via standards (IEEE 42010), textbook rigor (Munzner’s *Visualization Analysis and Design*), and methodological frameworks for measurable improvement of system-viz effectiveness in software engineering contexts.
