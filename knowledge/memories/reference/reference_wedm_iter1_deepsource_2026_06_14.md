## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Advanced Thermo-Electro-Mechanical Modeling and Process Physics in WEDM**

A world-leading expert in WEDM next masters the coupled thermo-electric, hydrodynamic, and plasma-channel physics that govern material removal, recast layer formation, wire vibration, and dielectric breakdown under high-energy pulsed conditions. This includes:

- Transient heat conduction with moving Gaussian heat source and phase-change boundary (melting/evaporation)
- Dielectric fluid ionization, plasma channel expansion, and pressure wave propagation
- Wire electrode dynamics: transverse vibration modes, tension modulation, and servo-controlled gap maintenance
- Subsurface metallurgical transformations, residual stress profiles, and white-layer metallurgy in aerospace alloys
- Pulse discrimination algorithms and adaptive iso-energy control strategies

**METHOD/THEORY:**

Finite-element solutions to the coupled heat-transfer and Maxwell equations with temperature-dependent material properties; explicit tracking of plasma flushing efficiency and debris particle transport; stochastic modeling of discharge probability density as function of gap voltage, contamination, and electrode surface state.

**STANDARDS & GOVERNMENT SOURCES:**

- ASME B5.64-2002 (Wire Electrical Discharge Machines – Terminology, Parameters, and Procedures)
- NIST Special Publication 960-12: “Measurement and Control of Wire EDM Process Dynamics” (2005)
- ISO 14137:2015 – Test conditions for wire electrical-discharge machines

**UNIVERSITY / TEXTBOOK LEVEL:**

- MIT OCW 2.008 (Design and Manufacturing II) – WEDM process physics module
- “Wire Electrical Discharge Machining” chapter in Kalpakjian & Schmid, Manufacturing Engineering and Technology, 7th ed. (2013) – thermal models and recast analysis
- “Electro-Physical and Chemical Processes” in textbooks such as McGeough, Advanced Methods of Machining (1988)

**CONFERENCE / SEMINAR DEPTH:**

- CIRP Annals papers on plasma channel radius evolution and crater geometry (e.g., Kunieda et al. series)
- North American Manufacturing Research Conference (NAMRC) proceedings on real-time wire lag compensation and thermal error mapping

This layer sits immediately above basic machine setup and first-order MRR understanding and is required for predictive modeling, closed-loop servo design, and safety-critical aerospace certification of WEDM processes.
