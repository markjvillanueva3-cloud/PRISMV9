A world-leading expert in compliance-safety must next master **quantitative refinement and compositional verification of hyperproperties**, moving beyond qualitative HyperLTL synthesis and epistemic logics into measurable assurance, probabilistic non-interference, and modular assurance-case algebra. This layer emphasizes metric-based hyperproperties, refinement calculi for hyperproperties, and standards-compliant quantitative argumentation.

Key next sources include:

- **Quantitative Hyperproperties and HyperLTL with distances**: Clarkson et al. (2022) develop hyperproperties over metric spaces, enabling quantitative notions of information-flow leakage and robustness (IEEE CSF 2022).
- **Refinement and composition**: Milushev & Clarke (2014, updated frameworks in later works) and later extensions in **Secure Refinement for Hyperproperties** (Mantel & Sands, 2021–2023 lineage) provide algebraic composition operators.
- **Assurance case metatheory**: Bloomfield et al. (2021) “Confidence Arguments in Assurance Cases” (Ada User Journal / UK MoD standards lineage) and the **ISO/IEC 15026-2:2022** (Part 2: Assurance case) specify structured confidence arguments and defeaters.
- **Probabilistic and relational verification**: Barthe et al. (2023) “A Relational Logic for Hyperproperties” (ESOP 2023 follow-on) and **McIver & Morgan’s** “Abstractions for Probabilistic Hyperproperties” (2022, LNCS).

- **NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 (2020)** Appendix J on Privacy and Assurance, cross-mapped to hyperproperty enforcement.
- **IEEE P7009 (2023 status)**: Standard for Fail-Safe Design of Autonomous and Semi-Autonomous Systems – quantitative safety argumentation requirements.

These provide the institutional depth (ISO 15026-2, NIST, IEEE), methodological rigor (quantitative refinement, compositional assurance), and theoretical foundation (metric hyperproperties, probabilistic relational Hoare logics) required for next-generation compliance-safety in PRISM’s assurance engines.
