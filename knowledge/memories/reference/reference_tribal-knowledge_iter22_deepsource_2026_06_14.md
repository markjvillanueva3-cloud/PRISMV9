## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next layer: knowledge-based view (KBV) of the firm integrated with information systems design for KMS in distributed software platforms.**

After the covered foundations in CoP, sensemaking, absorptive capacity, boundary objects, epistemic cultures, and stickiness, the subsequent mastery layer addresses how tacit tribal knowledge is converted into strategic organizational capability via explicit KBV mechanisms and engineered KMS architectures. This layer emphasizes capability orchestration, ontology-enabled knowledge representation, and governance of knowledge flows inside software engineering environments—precisely how platforms like PRISM must instrument experiential computing without reducing it to mere data.

Core elements include:
- KBV framing of knowledge as the primary productive resource, requiring integration and reconfiguration routines (Grant 1996).
- Design principles for KMS that resolve motivational, technical, and contextual barriers while supporting collective knowing-in-practice (Alavi & Leidner 2001).
- Operationalization of working knowledge through knowledge markets, repositories, and cultural practices that make invisible tribal expertise legible at enterprise scale (Davenport & Prusak 1998).

Institutional depth draws from strategic management literature on capability hierarchies, MIS Quarterly syntheses of IS-enabled KM, and textbook codifications of audit, mapping, and measurement methodologies. These extend the listed ISO 30401 requirements into platform-specific architecture decisions for semantic interoperability and memory infrastructures.
