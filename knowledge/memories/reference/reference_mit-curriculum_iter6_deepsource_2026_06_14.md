## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Hierarchical, Causal, and Multi-Modal Learner Modeling with Formal Cognitive Architectures**

A world-leading expert in mit-curriculum next masters the integration of hierarchical Bayesian cognitive models with causal inference frameworks and formal computational cognitive architectures. This layer moves beyond isolated skill tracing to structured, multi-scale learner representations that incorporate forgetting dynamics, relational skill graphs, and optimal experiment design under uncertainty.

Key next sources include:

- **Hierarchical and nonparametric extensions**: Work on infinite hidden Markov models and hierarchical Dirichlet processes for learner modeling that automatically discovers latent skill hierarchies without pre-specifying the number of skills.
- **Causal and counterfactual KT**: Formal causal graphs and do-calculus applied to knowledge tracing to distinguish correlation from causation in learning trajectories and enable counterfactual “what-if” interventions.
- **Integration with ACT-R / Soar-style cognitive architectures**: Mapping psychometric learner models onto production-rule cognitive architectures with declarative and procedural memory modules, allowing simulation of transfer and interference effects.
- **Multi-modal and sensor-augmented tracing**: Fusion of interaction logs with physiological or eye-tracking data using dynamic Bayesian networks and probabilistic programming.
- **Formal verification of learner models**: Use of probabilistic model checking (e.g., PRISM probabilistic model checker) to verify guarantees on mastery probability under different pedagogical policies.

These build directly on MIT’s computational cognitive science sequence and recent causal EDM/neurosymbolic advances while drawing from IEEE and ACM standards on adaptive learning systems.
