## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next-layer expertise in dormant-data** centers on audit/certification methodologies for long-term repositories, locality-aware coding for repair under sleepy synchrony, and migration frameworks that protect archival availability against both classical and quantum threats when clients remain dormant for decades.

ISO 16363:2012 supplies concrete, auditable criteria—organizational infrastructure, risk management, succession planning, and security controls—extending OAIS to certify that dormant clients can still obtain authentic, retrievable data long after original key holders or nodes have slept. This standard supplies the institutional checklist that composable UC proofs must ultimately satisfy in practice.

On the coding side, locally repairable codes (LRCs) improve the storage-vs-repair-bandwidth frontier of Rabin IDA and regenerating codes by guaranteeing that a failed fragment can be reconstructed by contacting only a small constant number of live nodes (Gopalan et al. 2012). The resulting locality parameter is essential for proactive erasure-coded archives operating in Pass–Shi-style fractional synchrony, where most parties may be dormant at any repair epoch.

Finally, NIST IR 8105 (2016) details concrete migration roadmaps and cryptoperiod management for post-quantum algorithms in archival settings, linking forward-secure signatures (already covered) to the concrete key- and algorithm-agility requirements that maintain long-horizon UC security when the global setup itself must evolve.

These three bodies of work—certification standards, locality theory, and quantum-migration practice—form the immediate next knowledge layer that integrates the already-mastered UC/dormant-party models, POR constructions, and OAIS reference architecture into deployable, auditable manufacturing-intelligence archival systems.
