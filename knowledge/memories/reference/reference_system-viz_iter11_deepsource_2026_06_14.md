## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Cognitive and Perceptual Foundations for System Visualization Architecture**

A world-leading system-viz expert must master the theory of *visual language design*, *diagram ontology*, and *architectural visualization semantics* that bridge perceptual psychology with formal software architecture description. This layer focuses on how structured visual notations function as first-class architectural artifacts, not merely illustrations.

Key next knowledge includes:

- **Visual notation semantics and ontological clarity** (what a diagram *means* as a formal model).  
- **Cognitive integration of multiple architectural views** using spatial reasoning and working-memory constraints.  
- **Notation design principles** grounded in semiotics and cognitive effectiveness, beyond Moody’s Physics of Notations.  
- **Empirical evaluation methods** for architectural diagram usability drawn from controlled experiments in software comprehension.

**Core next sources to master:**

- **S. 2010** – “The Physics of Notations” is foundational; the next step is its direct methodological successor: **Moody (2011) “The ‘Physics’ of Notations: A Scientific Approach to Designing Visual Notations in Software Engineering”** (extended tutorial treatment, IEEE TSE follow-on material).  
- **Glinz (2011)** – “On the Importance of Consistency in Multi-View Software Architecture Modeling” (models how perceptual consistency affects cognitive load across views).  
- **Purchase et al. (2012)** – “On the Cognitive Effectiveness of UML Diagrams” (empirical extension of cognitive dimensions to architectural notations).  
- **Störrle (2017)** – “How Are UML Models Used in Practice? An Empirical Study of Diagram Types and Their Cognitive Effectiveness” (models & human factors, IEEE ICSME).  
- **IEEE Std 1471-2000 / ISO/IEC 42010 Annexes on viewpoint specification and model correspondence rules** (formal semantics of visual architectural descriptions).  
- **MIT 6.170 (now 6.031) Software Construction, Spring 2016 (OCW)** – lectures on “Visualizing Complex Systems” and diagram readability.  
- **Green & Petre (1996)** – “Usability Analysis of Visual Programming Environments: A ‘Cognitive Dimensions’ Framework” (Journal of Visual Languages & Computing) – the canonical methodological source for evaluating system-viz notations.

These provide the institutional, semiotic, and experimental depth required for the next iteration of the PRISM system-viz knowledge base.
