## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Institutional Logics & Infrastructure in Software Architecture Description**

A world-leading expert in tribal-knowledge next masters the **micro-level mechanisms of institutional work** that produce and maintain “tribal knowledge” as an informal infrastructure inside large-scale software platforms. This layer moves beyond macro institutional complexity to the **practical, recursive, and material practices** by which engineers, architects, and platform teams enact, repair, and strategically forget architectural decisions, turning them into durable yet mutable organizational memory.

Key next sources focus on:
- **Institutional work** as day-to-day maintenance of conflicting logics in engineering practice (Lawrence & Suddaby 2006; Zietsma & Lawrence 2010).
- **Infrastructural inversion** and the invisible work of classification, boundary objects, and standards in software-intensive organizations (Bowker & Star 1999; Star & Ruhleder 1996).
- **Forgetting and selective remembering** as deliberate institutional strategies in technical memory systems (Anteby & Molnár already covered; see further de Holan & Phillips 2004; Martin de Holan 2011).
- **Sociomateriality** and the constitutive entanglement of code, architecture descriptions (ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 already covered), and organizational logics (Orlikowski 2007; Leonardi 2011).
- **Standards as relational infrastructure** in software engineering governance (Hanseth & Monteiro 1997; Star 2010).

These provide concrete methodological depth for analyzing how PRISM’s internal “tribal-knowledge galaxy” is actively performed, standardized, and infrastructurally embedded through tools, repositories, wikis, and architectural decision records.
