## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: MACHINING PROCESS PHYSICS & DYNAMICS (Safety-Critical Layer)**

A world-leading expert in milling moves beyond basic kinematics into the mechanistic modeling of cutting forces, process stability, and thermo-mechanical phenomena governed by workpiece material constitutive behavior, tool geometry, and regenerative chatter theory.

Core uncovered topics at this depth include:
- Mechanistic force modeling using oblique cutting transformations and unified mechanics (Altintas)
- Regenerative chatter stability lobes via frequency-domain and time-domain solutions, including semi-discretization
- Dynamic stiffness, modal analysis of spindle-tool-holder assemblies, and FRF measurement techniques
- Thermo-mechanical coupling, heat partition models, and temperature prediction in the shear zone and tool-chip interface
- Nonlinearities from process damping, runout, and variable tooth engagement in milling
- High-speed machining stability limits and the role of process damping at low radial immersions

**METHOD/THEORY/STANDARDS:**

- Altintas’s unified mechanics of cutting approach (predictive force coefficients from orthogonal-to-oblique transformation without relying on proprietary constants)
- Budak & Altintas (1998) analytical stability lobe theory for milling
- Time-domain simulation methods (Insperger & Stépán semi-discretization, 2002–2004)
- ASME B5.54 / ISO 230-1 / ISO 10791 series for machine tool performance and dynamic testing protocols
- NIST reports on machining process metrology and uncertainty in force and vibration measurement

This layer forms the foundation for safe, chatter-free parameter selection and virtual simulation of milling physics before any constants are applied.
