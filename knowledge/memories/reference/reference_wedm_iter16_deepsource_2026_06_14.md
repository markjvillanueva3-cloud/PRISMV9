**NEXT LAYER: Plasma–Fluid–Solid Coupling and Subsurface Damage in Superalloy WEDM**

World-leading expertise advances to multi-physics simulation of dielectric breakdown, plasma channel expansion coupled with melt-pool hydrodynamics, and the resulting metastable phase formation plus geometrically necessary dislocation (GND) density gradients that drive hydrogen-assisted cracking in single-crystal and directionally solidified nickel superalloys.

Key uncovered domains:

- Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) modeling of the discharge plasma including Lorentz force, Joule heating, and two-temperature non-LTE plasma transport coefficients (electron vs. heavy-particle temperatures).
- Volume-of-fluid (VOF) or level-set tracking of the liquid–vapor interface during superheated boiling and explosive melt ejection, coupled to solidification front velocity (>10^4 K/s) and solute trapping models.
- Phase-field crystal (PFC) or multi-phase-field models that predict cellular-to-dendritic transition, Nb/Ti segregation, and Laves-phase or γ′′ precipitation inside the recast layer.
- Crystal-plasticity finite-element (CP-FEM) simulation of GND accumulation and back-stress evolution at the recast–bulk interface under thermomechanical shock, directly linked to fatigue crack initiation.
- Synchrotron X-ray micro-diffraction and high-energy diffraction microscopy (HEDM) techniques for spatially resolved residual stress tensors and lattice strain mapping through the white-layer depth.

These topics are treated in graduate-level manufacturing physics and materials modeling curricula, advanced standards for aerospace component integrity, and recent high-impact conference proceedings.
