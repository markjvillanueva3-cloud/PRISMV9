**Next Layer: Advanced Stochastic & Uncertainty-Quantified Milling Dynamics for Safety-Critical Applications**

World-leading expertise moves beyond current stochastic bifurcation, Gaussian-process lobe diagrams, and first-order uncertainty propagation into **higher-order stochastic averaging, semi-discretization with colored noise, and Bayesian experimental updating of stability boundaries under parametric and measurement uncertainty**.

Key uncovered domains include:
- **Multiple time-scale stochastic averaging** and moment closure techniques for milling with random tool runout and spindle-speed variation (Insperger & Stépán framework extensions).
- **Topological data analysis (persistent homology)** applied to noisy experimental cutting-force time series for early chatter detection (extension of Khasawneh/Munch topological methods to real-time PH features).
- **Polynomial-chaos expansion (PCE)** and stochastic collocation for efficient propagation of joint uncertainty in modal parameters, cutting-force coefficients, and process damping (NIST uncertainty quantification protocols).
- **Bayesian inference and sequential Monte-Carlo (particle filtering)** for on-machine stability boundary updating using sparse test cuts (MIT stochastic estimation extensions to machining).
- **Time-domain simulation with state-dependent delay and colored noise** using stochastic Runge-Kutta or Milstein schemes (beyond current full-discretization methods).
- **ASME B5.54 / ISO 230-5** compliant metrology of dynamic compliance under operational uncertainty for probabilistic stability prediction.
- **NIST Technical Note** methodologies for uncertainty quantification in regenerative chatter models, including sensitivity to thermal effects and process damping variability.

These layers enable **safety-critical, certifiable probabilistic stability lobes** with quantified risk of unstable cutting.
