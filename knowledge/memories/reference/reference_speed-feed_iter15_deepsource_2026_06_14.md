## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Thermomechanical Coupling, Adiabatic Shear Banding (ASB) Instability Criteria, and Microstructure-Linked Damage in Speed-Feed Regimes**

World-leading expertise advances to **stability analysis frameworks** for coupled thermo-viscoplastic flow under extreme rates, focusing on perturbation methods for ASB initiation, non-local regularization of softening, and dislocation-density-based damage evolution tied to surface integrity in subtractive processes.

Key uncovered domains:

- **Linear perturbation analysis for thermoplastic instability** (Molinari & Clifton, 1987; Molinari, 1997): analytical criteria for critical strain-rate and temperature at onset of shear banding in rate- and temperature-sensitive metals, including titanium and nickel alloys.
- **Gradient-enhanced continuum damage mechanics** with length-scale regularization for mesh-independent ASB prediction (Peerlings et al., 1996; Geers et al., 2010): incorporation of nonlocal damage variables to capture localization bandwidth observed in high-speed machining.
- **Dislocation-density-based DRX + damage coupling** extending internal-state-variable models to include void nucleation at triple junctions during dynamic recrystallization under adiabatic conditions (Horstemeyer & Gokhale, 1999; Roters et al., 2019 update in DAMASK framework).
- **NIST-recommended V&V practices for coupled thermomechanical-metallurgical codes** at the “physics of failure” hierarchy level, emphasizing uncertainty quantification in localization metrics (NIST IR 8487, 2023).
- **ASME V&V 40-2018**: Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation for Medical Devices – adapted to manufacturing physics for safety-critical cutting (ASME V&V 40, 2018).

These layers enable predictive simulation of chip segmentation, white-layer formation, and residual stress without reliance on empirical constants, directly feeding PRISM’s speed-feed galaxy for real-time microstructure-aware process control.
