## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next layer: neural scaling laws for compute-optimal training, quantized PEFT extensions, and principle-driven alignment methods.**

World-leading ai-training experts next master empirical scaling laws that predict loss as a power-law function of model size, dataset size, and training compute. Kaplan et al. (2020) formalized these relationships from GPT-series ablations, enabling precise extrapolation of frontier-model behavior. Hoffmann et al. (2022) refined them into compute-optimal allocation (“Chinchilla laws”), demonstrating that previous parameter-heavy regimes were suboptimal and that data must scale roughly linearly with compute—directly informing training recipes for models beyond 100B parameters.

Building on covered LoRA and mixed-precision work, the layer incorporates 4-bit NormalFloat quantization fused with low-rank adapters. Dettmers et al. (2023) show this enables fine-tuning of 65B-parameter models on a single 48 GB GPU while preserving full-precision quality, closing the memory gap left by prior 8-bit optimizers.

For alignment beyond RLHF, experts study self-supervised constitutional principles. Bai et al. (2022) replace human preference labels with AI critique against a short list of explicit principles, yielding harmlessness via iterated RL without additional human data.

Standards depth adds ISO/IEC 23894:2023, which supplies a full risk-management process model tailored to AI development lifecycles (including training-data curation, hyperparameter search, and emergent-capability evaluation), extending the management-system requirements of ISO/IEC 42001.

These topics appear in graduate ML-systems syllabi (e.g., follow-on modules to CMU 10-605) and NeurIPS/ICML tutorials on frontier training.
