## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Hierarchical, Causal, and Multi-scale Models for Knowledge Tracing and Optimal Curriculum Planning**

A world-leading expert in mit-curriculum next masters **hierarchical Bayesian models**, **causal discovery in student modeling**, **partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) for curriculum optimization**, and **graph-theoretic representations of skill structures** at multiple granularities. This layer emphasizes formal verification of learning policies, integration of cognitive architectures with probabilistic programming, and the use of relational and temporal point-process models beyond standard BKT/DKT.

Key next-layer sources include:

- **Bayesian hierarchical extensions** that place hyperpriors on skill transition and guess/slip parameters to share statistical strength across skills and populations (Wilson & Kitzelmann, 2020).
- **Attentive and context-aware forgetting mechanisms** that replace static decay with dynamic, input-dependent temporal models (Lee & Yeung, 2022; Ghosh et al., 2020).
- **Graph-based and relational models** that treat the skill graph as a first-class latent variable learned via graph neural networks or relational GCNs, enabling automatic discovery of prerequisite structures (Nakagawa et al., 2019; Pardos et al., 2023).
- **Causal extensions to knowledge tracing** that distinguish correlation from intervention effects using do-calculus and counterfactual reasoning (Lee et al., 2021).
- **Optimal experimental design and POMDP planning** for selecting the next learning activity that maximizes information gain or expected learning gain under cognitive models (Zhang et al., 2022).
- **Integration with computational cognitive architectures** that embed ACT-R-style production rules inside probabilistic inference engines, allowing verification of curriculum policies via probabilistic model checkers (Kwiatkowska et al., 2011; MIT 6.804/9.66).

These build directly on the listed covered works by adding explicit hierarchy, causality, graph structure learning, and formal verification methods drawn from computational cognitive science, machine learning, and formal methods.
