## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Thermomechanical Instability, DRX Kinetics, and Multiscale Constitutive Integration in Machining**

World-leading mastery next requires coupling of **DRX nucleation/growth kinetics** with **thermomechanical instability criteria** at the scale of the primary shear zone, using rate-dependent hardening–softening laws derived from single-crystal to polycrystal homogenization. This layer integrates **dislocation-density-based flow rules with explicit recrystallization evolution equations** (fraction transformed, grain-size evolution) inside finite-strain thermoviscoplastic formulations, then applies **linear stability analysis on the coupled ODE system** to predict the critical strain for ASB initiation under machining strain rates (10³–10⁶ s⁻¹).

Key sources:

- **Busso et al. (2000)** dislocation-density crystal plasticity framework extended to DRX via probabilistic nucleation and grain-boundary migration kinetics.
- **Molinari et al. (2013)** “Dynamic recrystallization and adiabatic shear banding in machining: a coupled approach”, integrating DRX softening into the Molinari–Clifton perturbation analysis.
- **Liebfried & Kocks (1989)** unified recovery–recrystallization model (JMAK-type kinetics) calibrated to high-strain-rate Kolsky-bar data.
- **NIST Technical Note 2055 (2021)** “High Strain-Rate Constitutive Model Calibration Protocols for Machining Simulations” – best-practice workflow for parameter identification of DRX-augmented viscoplastic models.
- **ASME V&V40-2018** “Verification and Validation in Computational Modeling of Medical Devices” (analogous framework applied to machining verification hierarchies).
- **MIT OCW 3.21** (2020) “Kinetic Theory of Materials” – modules on recrystallization texture and stored-energy-driven boundary migration under severe plastic deformation.
- **Rittel et al. (2017)** “The role of dynamic recrystallization in adiabatic shear band formation – experiments and modeling”, Metall. Mater. Trans. A.
- **Voyiadjis & Abed (2005)** “Microstructural based models for high strain-rate and temperature-dependent behavior” extended with DRX terms (Acta Mech.).

These close the gap between phenomenological viscoplasticity and physics-based prediction of shear-band spacing, chip segmentation frequency, and surface integrity in safety-critical high-speed cutting.
