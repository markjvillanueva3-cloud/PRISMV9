**Next Layer: Heterogeneous Knowledge Transformation via Institutions, Fibred Semantics, and Mechanized Semantic Interoperability**

A world-leading expert in knowledge-conversion next masters **institution theory** as the abstract model-theoretic foundation for translating between heterogeneous logical systems and ontologies without collapsing their individual semantics. This extends prior categorical and algebraic-graph work by providing rigorous “forgetful” and “free” functors between institutions, enabling provably correct knowledge lifting and lowering.

They further internalize **fibred semantics and Grothendieck institutions** (Goguen & Burstall 1992 extended by Diaconescu 2008), which supply the categorical glue for multi-logic and multi-paradigm knowledge bases. This layer directly supports safe, modular conversion rules across differing expressivity levels.

At the standards and tooling level, the expert commands **ISO/IEC 24744:2017 methodology metamodeling** combined with **OMG MOF 2.5 QVT** operational and relational semantics, plus the **Theory of Institutions** mechanization in theorem provers (e.g., Hets – Heterogeneous Tool Set).

They study **delta-based bidirectional transformation** semantics lifted to institutional level (Diskin et al. 2011, extended in Macedo et al. 2019 “Delta-based Model Transformation” and “Least Change Bidirectional Model Transformation”), providing mechanized delta lenses that guarantee Hippocraticness and history ignorance for knowledge conversion.

Finally, they master **semantic refactoring and ontology evolution** through **categorical model management** (Diskin 2016 “Model Management Reloaded” and “Algebraic Model Management” frameworks), including the use of **adjoint functors for view-update** problems in knowledge bases.

This layer supplies the institutional and fibred machinery required to move beyond single-logic refinement or graph rewriting into verifiable, heterogeneous, multi-institution knowledge conversion engines suitable for PRISM-scale semantic lifting.
