## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
The next mastery layer in token-optimization centers on disjunctive programming, Benders decomposition for structured MIPs, and corner-polyhedra theory with subadditive cut-generating functions. These supply rigorous polyhedral and decomposition methodologies that strengthen extended formulations and enable scalable algorithmic separation beyond covered lift-and-project, Sherali-Adams, Lasserre, and column-generation techniques.

Balas (1979) formalizes disjunctive programming at Carnegie Mellon as a convex-analytic framework for deriving valid inequalities from logical OR conditions on polyhedra, directly supporting automated cut generation in branch-and-cut. Benders (1962) introduces variable partitioning for mixed-integer problems, creating master-subproblem iteration that exploits block structure (common in stochastic and network MIPs) and underpins logic-based extensions. Gomory & Johnson (1972) develop subadditive functions over corner polyhedra (IBM/University of Kentucky), yielding a complete characterization of valid inequalities for group problems and inspiring MIR, lift-and-project, and Gomory mixed-integer cuts implemented in modern solvers. Jünger et al. (2010) survey these threads with institutional depth from decades of DOE- and NSF-supported polyhedral research.

These appear in graduate polyhedral combinatorics seminars and large-scale optimization reports focused on high-performance solver architecture.
