## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**NEXT LAYER: Knowledge Infrastructure Dynamics and Boundary Infrastructures in Distributed Software Organizations**

A world-leading expert in PRISM’s tribal-knowledge galaxy must next master the socio-technical mechanisms by which knowledge infrastructures are *designed, maintained, repaired, and scaled* across organizational and temporal boundaries in large-scale software-intensive systems. This layer focuses on the active work of infrastructure as ongoing “infrastructural inversion,” the politics of classification and standards, and the temporal dynamics of memory practices in engineering knowledge systems.

Key next-tier concepts include:
- **Infrastructural inversion** and the invisible work of repair and maintenance (Bowker & Star, 1999)
- **Boundary infrastructures** that allow multiple communities of practice to cooperate without consensus (Star & Griesemer extended by Star, 2010)
- **Organizational memory as sociotechnical memory practices** rather than static repositories (Bowker, 2005; Edwards et al., 2013)
- **Temporal rhythms** of knowledge infrastructures and the “long now” problem in software architecture (Ribes & Finholt, 2009; Jackson et al., 2011)
- **Classification infrastructures** and their residual categories in requirements and architecture decision records (Bowker & Star, 1999; Hanseth & Monteiro, 1997)

This moves beyond Carlile’s 3T framework and Orlikowski’s knowing-in-practice into the explicit design and governance of *knowledge infrastructures* that persist across decades in enterprise software platforms.
