## Next-layer knowledge (Hermes-drafted)
**Next Layer: Cognitive and Perceptual Foundations for System Visualization at Architectural Scale**

A world-leading expert in system-viz next masters the integration of **cognitive load theory**, **perceptual organization (Gestalt + pre-attentive processing)**, and **diagram-specific visual reasoning models** drawn from software architecture, information visualization, and human-computer interaction literature. This layer focuses on how architects and engineers actually parse, navigate, and reason with large-scale polymetric, hierarchical, and relational system diagrams under realistic working-memory constraints.

Key uncovered areas include:
- **Cognitive dimensions of notations** extended to system-scale views (beyond Purchase 2014), specifically Green’s original framework applied to architecture description languages.
- **Pre-attentive visual properties** and their measured effectiveness for software architecture element detection (color, form, motion, spatial grouping).
- **Working-memory chunking** strategies in diagrammatic reasoning for complex software systems (building on Baddeley 2012 and Cowan 2001 but applied to IEEE 42010 viewpoints).
- **Gestalt principles** formalized for software visualization layout and the limits of perceptual organization when rendering hundreds of components.

- Larkin JH, Simon HA (1987) Why a diagram is (sometimes) worth ten thousand words. Cognitive Science 11(1).
