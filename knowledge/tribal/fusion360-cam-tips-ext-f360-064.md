# Lead and Lag Angles for 5-Axis Surface Quality

Lead angle tilts the tool forward in the direction of travel; lag tilts it backward. For finishing with ball nose tools, use 10-15 degrees of lead to shift the contact point away from the tool tip, where surface speed approaches zero. Lag is useful when cutting into corners where a forward-leaning tool would gouge the adjacent wall. Never exceed 20 degrees of lead on thin-walled parts — the off-axis force component increases deflection significantly.
