#Obsidian as a Second Brain for AI-Assisted Development

Cross-referenced against PRISM's 770-entry wiki at H:/prism/knowledge/wiki/ (575 engines + 96 dispatchers + 99 memories), the Karpathy LLM-Wiki doctrine in WIKI_SCHEMA.md, and the auto-injected memory layer at knowledge/memories/{user,feedback,project,reference}/.

#§1 — PARA vs Zettelkasten: When to Use Which (and What PRISM Already Does)

#1.1 PARA (Tiago Forte) — organize by actionability

PARA buckets ALL information into four categories distinguished by time-horizon and outcome:

  • Projects — short-term efforts with a deadline ("complete webpage design", "buy a new computer")
  • Areas — ongoing responsibilities with no end date ("finances", "writing", "health")
  • Resources — topics of interest for future use ("graphic design", "Japanese language")
  • Archives — inactive items from any of the three other buckets

Forte's central principle: organize "according to the projects and goals you are committed to right now" — not by broad subject like in school. He explicitly contrasts areas vs projects with the question: "Does 'strategic planning' ever end for good?" Areas persist indefinitely; projects close out.

PARA's strength is frictionless capture. Anything new lands in one of four buckets in <5 seconds. Its weakness for code wikis: it organizes by who-will-act-on-this, not by what-is-this-conceptually. A page describing the Kienzle force model has no "project" or "area" — it's a durable concept.

#1.2 Zettelkasten (Niklas Luhmann) — organize by atomic concept + link

Luhmann's slip-box (~90,000 cards, fueled 70 books + 400 articles) had two rules that survive: atomicity (one idea per card) and hyper-textuality (each card carries a unique ID and links to others). The fleeting → literature → permanent taxonomy is not Luhmann's — it was retro-fitted by Sönke Ahrens in How to Take Smart Notes (2017):

  • Fleeting notes — quick captures while doing something else, discarded within a day or two after reprocessing
  • Literature notes — bibliographic references + key excerpts, stored in a reference manager
  • Permanent notes ("Zettel") — self-contained, written-as-if-for-print, never discarded, in your own words

Zettelkasten's strength for code wikis: every concept becomes a durable, link-rich node that compounds across projects. Its weakness: no actionability layer. A pure Zettelkasten doesn't tell you what to work on next.

#1.3 What PRISM does (hybrid, leans Zettelkasten)

PRISM's H:/prism/knowledge/wiki/ is a structural hybrid that maps cleanly to neither paradigm:

  • The 11 subdirectories (concepts/, entities/, decisions/, patterns/, trajectories/, summaries/, code-tribal/, architecture/, software-engineering/, ux-design/, lessons/) are categories by note-type — closer to Steph Ango's "organize by type-of-note, then link" school than to either PARA or Zettel.
  • 770 entries are predominantly atomic concept-pages (575 engines, one per *Engine.ts) — Zettelkasten-shaped.
  • The decisions/ and trajectories/ directories carry an actionability layer that PARA would call "Projects" — synthesis outputs from Creative Reasoning + AGI runs, dated and time-bounded.
  • The auto-injected memory files in knowledge/memories/{user,feedback,project,reference}/ are PARA-shaped: project/ ≈ Projects, reference/ ≈ Resources, user/ ≈ Areas (cross-cutting), feedback/ ≈ Archives + active learnings.

Hybrid recommendation for code wikis:

  1. Atomic concept pages by directory-type (Zettelkasten core) — every engine, dispatcher, formula, decision is its own page.
  2. PARA layer on top via frontmatter category: + auto-injected memory split — Projects (active work), Areas (ongoing concerns like SVI, omega), Resources (durable docs), Archives (deprecated engines).
  3. MOC layer for navigation (Nick Milo, see §4) — top-level entry points like wiki/index.md and per-domain hubs.

PRISM already does (1) and (2); §4 covers what's missing on (3).

#§2 — Atomic Note Principles (Quote the Authors)

#2.1 Andy Matuschak — "Evergreen notes should be atomic"

"It's a heuristic: write notes such that each can be understood — and ideally, used — without referring to many others. This way, it's easier to form connections across topics and contexts." — Matuschak, Evergreen notes should be atomic

Matuschak frames atomicity as a separation-of-concerns analogue from software engineering: modules should focus on one thing, so they're more easily reusable. He acknowledges the tension explicitly:

"There's no clear litmus test or correct answer here — just a bunch of tradeoffs."

The two failure modes:

  • Too broad: "you might not notice when you encounter some new idea about one of the notions contained within, and links to that note will be muddied."
  • Too fragmented: "you'll also fragment your link network, which may make it harder to see certain connections."

#2.2 Concept-oriented (not source-oriented)

"It's not just about accumulation. There's also no pressure to synthesize your new ideas on the concept with your prior thoughts about it." — Matuschak, Evergreen notes should be concept-oriented

Organize by the underlying concept, not by the source (book, project, meeting). When the same concept comes up across three books, all three add to the same concept-page — surfacing contradictions and synthesis opportunities at write-time, not query-time.

#2.3 Densely linked

"Finding the right links requires reading old notes, so it's also an organic mechanism for intermittently reviewing" — Matuschak, Evergreen notes should be densely linked

Five functions of dense linking per Matuschak:

  1. Deeper learning (elaborative encoding)
  2. Organic review of old notes
  3. Documenting how conclusions were reached
  4. Many paths to navigate idea-space
  5. Attention management while drafting

#2.4 Knowledge work should accrete

"We should strive to design practices systems which yield compounding returns on our efforts as they accumulate over time. We should do our serious thinking in the form of Evergreen notes so that the thinking accumulates."

This is the same principle the Karpathy LLM-Wiki applies (WIKI_SCHEMA.md §1): "knowledge compiles once and stays current. The wiki is a compounding artifact."

#3.2 DataviewJS — the queryable knowledge graph

DataviewJS turns the vault into a queryable database. Examples from the official docs:

DQL — list all notes with a tag, sorted:

TABLE rating AS "Rating", summary AS "Summary"
FROM #games
SORT rating DESC

DataviewJS — group by category, filter by metadata:

let pages = dv.pages("#books and -#books/finished").where(b => b.rating >= 7);
for (let group of pages.groupBy(b => b.genre)) {
    dv.header(3, group.key);
    dv.list(group.rows.file.name);
}

#3.3 Applied to PRISM's wiki (hypothetical queries)

// All concept-pages with confidence < 0.7 (need human verification)
dv.pages('"knowledge/wiki/concepts"')
  .where(p => p.confidence < 0.7)
  .sort(p => p.last_verified)
  .file.link

// All Ollama-authored pages without verified_by stamp (CRITICAL per WIKI_SCHEMA.md §5)
dv.pages('"knowledge/wiki"')
  .where(p => p.author === 'ollama' && p.confidence > 0.6 && !p.verified_by)

// Engines with zero inbound wikilinks (orphans)
dv.pages('"knowledge/wiki/concepts"')
  .where(p => dv.pages().file.outlinks.path.includes(p.file.path) === false)

This is exactly what WikiLintEngine does today in PRISM (per WIKI_SCHEMA.md §3.3) — orphan detection, broken-cross-ref detection, stale-claim detection, physics-drift detection. Dataview is the user-facing equivalent of that engine.

2026 caveat: the Obsidian 2026 vault best-practices summary notes Dataview has been deprecated in favor of "Obsidian Bases" for native structure. Bases use the same frontmatter contract, so PRISM's frontmatter already future-proofs the lint engine.

#§4 — Maps of Content (MOC) — When to Build, When to Skip

#4.1 What an MOC is (Nick Milo, LYT framework)

A Map of Content is a special note that, instead of containing detail itself, contains wikilinks pointing to other notes on the same topic. Per Obsidian Rocks: MOCs function "as a living table of contents that is continuously updated and always reflects the actual state of your vault." They are organic, hand-curated, and "infinitely flexible — you can add one note to multiple maps, or to none."

#4.2 When to make one — the "mental squeeze point"

"Any time you start to feel overwhelmed by how many notes you're trying to juggle, create a map of content."

Nick Milo's heuristic: build an MOC at the mental squeeze point — when the number of notes on a topic crosses the threshold where you can't hold them all in your head. Not earlier (premature taxonomy), not later (knowledge becomes unfindable).

The LYT ARC framework: Add → Relate → Communicate. MOCs sit at "Relate" — they're how you wire atomic notes into navigable hubs.

#4.3 PRISM's MOC story (mostly missing)

PRISM has one MOC today: wiki/index.md (770 entries, auto-generated by WikiIndexMaintainerEngine). This is a catalog MOC — flat, comprehensive, but not curated by topic.

What's missing — per-domain hubs:

  • wiki/concepts/_force-physics-moc.md — entry point to all force/torque/specific-energy concept pages
  • wiki/architecture/_dispatcher-moc.md — map of all 97 dispatchers + their action-counts + cross-refs
  • wiki/code-tribal/_hypermill-moc.md — entry point to the hyperMILL strategy clusters (face, contour, pencil, equidistant, lace, thread-milling, …)
  • wiki/lessons/_cad-fusion-live-moc.md — chronological narrative of the milestone's lesson notes

These would be Nick Milo MOCs: hand-curated (or LLM-curated and human-reviewed), durable, and grow as concepts accrete.

#4.4 Anti-pattern: taxonomy bloat

The forum threads on MOCs warn explicitly: don't make an MOC for every directory. The signal that an MOC is needed is user friction, not file-count. If a domain has 5 notes and you're not lost, no MOC. If a domain has 50 notes and you can't remember where the canonical entry-point is, build one.

For PRISM: build MOCs only when a /wiki-query pass against a topic returns >10 results AND the chat reads >3 of them to answer. That's the squeeze point.

#5.2 Smart Connections (the dominant choice — 786K downloads)

Per the official repo: Smart Connections runs a local embedding model (default: nomic-embed-text, 768 dim, ~30 MB for 10K notes) and exposes two views:

  • Connections View — opens with a note, shows semantically related notes
  • Lookup View — semantic search over the whole vault

Acknowledged limitations from the docs:

"Semantic queries do not behave like plain text search. A note containing the exact query text might not appear if not semantically similar."

Friction points (from production users):

  • Mobile sync requires .smart-env/ to be ignored by third-party sync tools
  • Multi-provider routing moved to a separate Smart Chat Pro plugin — basic plugin is embeddings-only
  • Embedding quality depends on note hygiene — template noise, attachments, low-content stubs all degrade results

#5.3 ChatGPT MD — markdown-as-conversation

ChatGPT MD treats every conversation as a markdown file with frontmatter:

---
model: gpt-5
system_commands: ['act as a senior javascript developer']
temperature: 0.7
openaiUrl: https://api.openai.com
---

The chat is the file body, edits roll forward, and per-note model-switching is a one-line frontmatter change. Recent versions added:

  • Agents (v3.1.0) — reusable AI personas as markdown files with frontmatter + system-prompt body
  • Vault tools (v3.0.0) — vault-search, file-read, web-search via Brave API, all human-in-the-loop

Ollama integration: ollama pull llama3.2 then set the model name. No API key, no cloud roundtrip, no rate limit.

This is the same stack PRISM already runs. OllamaHookBridgeEngine routes summarize/explain/classify to qwen2.5-coder:7b; the wiki schema mandates ≥70% of maintenance tokens go to Ollama.

#5.5 Friction patterns (industry-wide)

  1. Embedding noise — slow searches stem from indexing too many low-quality notes. Fix: exclude templates, attachments, daily-notes from index.
  2. Memory pressure — Ollama loads/unloads models, so the first query after model-switch is slow. Fix: pin one chat model + one embed model.
  3. Connection issues — usually verify Ollama on localhost:11434 is reachable.
  4. Stale embeddings — re-embed when note content drifts >20% (Smart Connections does this on save automatically).

#§6 — Daily Notes + Atomic Capture (Fleeting → Permanent Flow)

Sönke Ahrens' three-stage flow: Fleeting → Literature → Permanent.

For a PRISM development session this maps to:

#6.2 Bake into PRISM's session handoff system

PRISM already has the lifecycle infrastructure — per-agent-handoff.mjs, precompact-handoff.mjs, the Stop hook — but the fleeting → permanent promotion step is implicit, not explicit.

Recommendation: extend the /handoff skill to:

  1. Surface fleeting candidates — diff session against wiki/lessons/ and propose promotions for any error→recovery pattern that recurred.
  2. Auto-cite literature — every promoted lesson page must cite the engine, dispatcher, or formula it touches (already enforced by WikiLintEngine).
  3. Schema-promote permanent — bump confidence and stamp verified_by: claude-{id} when promoting Ollama-drafted notes.

This makes the "Knowledge work should accrete" principle (Matuschak §2.4) mechanically true: every session's friction becomes a wiki entry the next session reads.

#6.3 Daily-notes pattern (Obsidian default → PRISM analog)

Obsidian's "Daily Notes" plugin creates one note per day, dated YYYY-MM-DD.md. The wiki's trajectories/{YYYY-MM-DD}/{taskId}.md already does this — and the WIKI_SCHEMA §5 retention policy ("trajectories keeps 30 days at full fidelity, then summarized into a decisions/ page") is a mechanical fleeting → permanent flow. This is the same pattern Obsidian users build manually with templates; PRISM has it codified in an engine.

#§7 — Vault-to-Code Synergy (Keeping Wiki + Code in Sync)

#7.1 The drift problem

PRISM's wiki has 575 engine-entries, one per *Engine.ts. Every time an engine ships, gets renamed, or gets deleted, the wiki entry must update. Without automation, this drifts within weeks.

#7.2 What PRISM already does (auto-update on git events)

  • WikiIndexMaintainerEngine rebuilds index.md from live src/engines/ scan on bootstrap (770 entries last bootstrap 2026-05-08).
  • stop_on_unwired_assets.mjs Stop hook HARD BLOCKS commits where new engines exist without wiki entries.
  • build-state-snapshot.mjs auto-injects BUILT vs NEEDS_WIRING vs NEEDS_BUILDING vs NEEDS_FRONTEND on SessionStart.
  • WikiLintEngine detects physics-drift (page asserts a constant disagreeing with src/physics/constants.ts by >2%) and flags CRITICAL.

This is more aggressive than what Obsidian users do manually — most Obsidian vaults rely on human discipline. PRISM has hooks.

#7.3 What's missing

  • Engine-rename propagation — when EngineX becomes EngineY, the wiki page filename should rename and inbound [[wikilinks]] should rewrite. PRISM has /rename for code but not for wiki sync.
  • Deprecation sweep — when an engine is removed, the wiki page should move to wiki/archive/ not just orphan-flag.
  • Test-coverage backref — the wiki page for an engine should auto-show the count of __tests__/X.test.ts files. Currently you have to grep.

#7.4 The "git commit triggers wiki update" pattern

A post-commit hook that does:

  1. Diff src/engines/ → enumerate added/renamed/removed engines
  2. For each addition: ensure wiki/concepts/{Name}.md exists, append to index.md, append log.md entry
  3. For each rename: rewrite filename + scan [[wikilinks]] across vault for old name
  4. For each removal: move page to wiki/archive/, append log.md removal entry

PRISM has the engines (WikiIndexMaintainerEngine, WikiLogAppenderEngine). It's missing the post-commit wiring. One unit of work would close this gap.

#§8 — Anti-Patterns (and How Practitioners Avoid Them)

#8.1 Taxonomy graveyards

"Tag hygiene catches near-duplicate tags and single-use tags; tags are supposed to be a lightweight taxonomy, but when you have 200 tags with 150 used exactly once, they're noise." — common Obsidian forum diagnosis

Avoidance:

  • Controlled vocabulary — maintain a master tag list in an index file. New tags require justification.
  • Tag-merge passes — quarterly, merge near-duplicates (#cad, #cad/, #CAD → #cad).
  • Pluralize categories consistently — Steph Ango's rule: "Always pluralize categories and tags."

PRISM analog: the WIKI_SCHEMA §8 naming conventions enforce kebab-case + categories-must-match-directory-names. The lint engine flags drift.

#8.2 Folder hell

"Avoid folders for organization, and instead use internal links profusely." — Steph Ango

The 2026 best-practice consensus has shifted: folders for note-type, not topic. Topics belong in tags + links + MOCs.

PRISM does this — wiki/concepts/, wiki/entities/, wiki/decisions/ are types, not topics. ✓

#8.3 Link rot

External links rot — a link to a 2024 blog post is dead by 2026. Per Ben Congdon, the practical fix is archive every external URL to the Wayback Machine via cron. He built wayback-archiver (Rust CLI):

find . -name "*.md" -exec grep -Eo "(http|https)://[a-zA-Z0-9./?=_%:-]*" {} + |
  sort | uniq | wayback-archiver --out archive.json --merge

PRISM analog: none today. The wiki has hundreds of citations to engines/formulas (internal links — survive code changes via WikiLintEngine) but external citations have no archival. One cron job would close this.

#8.4 MOC bloat

The MOC anti-pattern is MOCs for every topic. The squeeze-point heuristic prevents this: build an MOC only when you can't navigate without one. Premature MOCs are worse than missing ones — they imply structure that doesn't yet exist.

PRISM has the opposite problem: one giant MOC (index.md, 770 entries) and zero per-domain MOCs. Fix per §4.3 — build MOCs only at the squeeze-point.

#8.5 Untouched / orphan notes

Notes that no one ever links to and no one ever re-reads are dead weight. Matuschak's principle: dense linking is also "an organic mechanism for intermittently reviewing." If a note has zero inbound links after 30 days, it's a candidate for either: (a) deletion, (b) merge into a related concept page, (c) promotion to MOC entry-point.

PRISM analog: WikiLintEngine flags BROKEN-orphan notes. But there's no auto-merge or auto-archive. A sweep skill would close this — /wiki-orphan-sweep proposes merges for the human to approve.

#8.6 The "second brain becomes a museum" failure

The biggest meta-anti-pattern across the PKM community: people invest 100 hours building a vault, then never query it. Symptoms:

  • Search returns results but the user keeps re-researching online
  • The vault is always being "reorganized" and never used
  • Every new note feels like overhead

The fix in 2026 is make the vault a query-first surface (Smart Connections, ChatGPT MD, RAG plugins). PRISM is structurally ahead here: the wiki is already the query surface (/wiki-query), and the auto-injected memory layer means Claude reads the relevant wiki entries on every keyword match without the user having to ask.

#§10 — Concrete Recommendations for PRISM (5 Units of Work)

In rough impact-order, smallest-first:

  1. U-WIKI-WAYBACK-CRON (~1 hr) — register cron that greps external URLs from knowledge/wiki/, archives to Wayback. Closes §8.3.
  2. U-WIKI-RENAME-PROPAGATE (~2 hr) — extend /rename skill to update wiki page filename + rewrite inbound [[wikilinks]]. Closes §7.3.
  3. U-WIKI-ORPHAN-SWEEP (~3 hr) — new skill /wiki-orphan-sweep that proposes merges for orphan notes 30+ days old. Closes §8.5.
  4. U-WIKI-MOC-BUILDER (~4 hr) — new engine that detects squeeze-points (>10 wiki-query results on same topic, chat reads >3) and proposes MOC creation. Closes §4.3.
  5. U-WIKI-FLEETING-PROMOTE (~5 hr) — extend /handoff to surface fleeting → permanent promotion candidates per §6.2. Highest leverage — makes "knowledge accretes" mechanically true.
