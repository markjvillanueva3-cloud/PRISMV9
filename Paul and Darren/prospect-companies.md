# Prospect Companies — Tier-Ranked
**For JM Die outbound business development**
**Compiled:** 2026-05-09  ·  Source: PRISM customer-corpus + public M&A research

> Tiering logic: **Tier A** = procurement window (recent ownership change, PE-backed roll-up, active acquirer) AND product fit. **Tier B** = sibling subsidiary of an existing JM customer — easy intro via "your sister-company already buys from us." **Tier C** = market-adjacent, plausible but cold.

## TIER A — Procurement window prospects (start here)

### 1. MW Components / Rosebank Industries (post-close)
- **Why now:** American Securities sold MW Components to **Rosebank Industries** in a $3.25B deal (announced Q1 2026, closing 2026). New owner = procurement re-evaluation window.
- **Sister-company hook:** JM already serves Elgin Fastener Group products (now MW-owned since Sep 2023).
- **MW siblings JM should quote:**
  - **Accurate Screw Machine** — precision threaded fasteners
  - **Ideal Fasteners** — stock + custom threaded fasteners
  - **RAF Electronic Hardware** — electronic spacers, standoffs
  - **Western Wire Products** — wire forms (matches JM's wire-EDM + lathe stack)
  - **Century Spring** — compression / extension springs
  - **Fox Valley Spring** — wire forms
- **Lead-in angle:** "We already make production parts for your Elgin sister-company; we're set up to serve the rest of MW's specialty-fastener portfolio without ramp-up."
- **Suggested contact search:** *"Director of Procurement" OR "VP Operations" Rosebank Industries -OR- MW Components* on LinkedIn.

### 2. Birmingham Fastener (Stephens Group portfolio)
- **Why now:** Aggressive roll-up — acquired Atlanta Rod (2020), Steel City Bolt (2021), K-T Bolt (2022), Champion Manufacturing (2022), Pacific Coast Bolt. $334M revenue 2023. Each acquired plant runs short on tooling/die capacity.
- **Existing relationship:** 78 files in JM's archive — small but active.
- **Lead-in angle:** "We've supplied tooling for your Birmingham operation; we can quote die work for the K-T Bolt and Steel City Bolt operations on the same lead-time."
- **Suggested contact search:** *Birmingham Fastener Director of Manufacturing OR VP Operations.*

### 3. Park-Ohio Holdings — Supply Technologies (NASDAQ:PKOH)
- **Why now:** Net sales $775.8M 2024, growing aerospace/defense. Supply Technologies runs 70+ distribution centers and contracts out a meaningful share of fastener manufacture.
- **JM has zero direct exposure to PKOH** — pure greenfield.
- **PKOH brands JM should quote:**
  - **RB&W Corp** (cold-formed automotive, Ohio)
  - **Arden Industrial Products**
  - **Arcon Fastener Corp**
  - **Delo Screw Products**
  - **Columbia Nut & Bolt**
- **Lead-in angle:** "We make production tooling for fastener brands in your distribution channel (Optimas, Holo-Krome, Fontana). We'd like to quote contract-mfg work for RB&W or Arden Industrial."
- **Suggested contact search:** *Park-Ohio Supply Technologies Director Sourcing Cleveland OR fastener procurement.*

### 4. Howmet Aerospace (NYSE:HWM) — formerly Alcoa Fastening Systems
- **Why now:** Spun out of Arconic 2020, has been actively rationalizing supply base. Aerospace fastener volumes recovering with commercial aviation rebound.
- **Existing relationship:** ARCONIC (predecessor brand) appears in JM customer list.
- **Lead-in angle:** "We supplied tooling under the Arconic relationship; we want to be on Howmet's bid list for cold-headed aerospace fastener tooling."
- **Suggested contact search:** *Howmet Aerospace Procurement Director Fastening Systems.*

### 5. TransDigm Group — Allfast Fastening Systems (NYSE:TDG)
- **Why now:** TransDigm continues its acquisition-led growth. Allfast (already a JM customer at 162 files) is one of dozens of TDG aerospace component subsidiaries.
- **TDG aerospace siblings JM should quote:**
  - **Aero-Instruments**
  - **Schroth Safety Products**
  - **AdelWiggins Group** (clamps, Tennessee — aerospace tooling fits JM)
- **Lead-in angle:** "We make Allfast's rivet die tooling already; we'd like to expand to other TransDigm aerospace component sub-fabricators that buy similar tooling."

## Conscious omissions (prospects we did NOT add and why)

- **Direct competitors of JM in the cold-heading-die niche** — they may co-quote rather than buy.
- **Automotive OEMs (Ford, GM, etc.)** — too far up the value chain; tier-2 fastener buyers are the right target.
- **Generic CNC job-shops** — no procurement window; JM should compete for their business not pitch them.

## Two flags before sending

1. **Verify ISO/AS9100 certification status** before claiming aerospace credentials in any email to Tier-A aerospace targets (Howmet, LISI, B&B, PCC, TriMas). Saying "ISO 9001-aligned discipline" is honest if the formal cert is in progress; saying "AS9100D certified" without the cert is fatal to the relationship.

2. **Cross-check sister-company claims** — when the email says "we already serve your sister company X," confirm X is genuinely active on JM's books in the last 24 months. The 24,545-program archive includes legacy work; some of those customer relationships may be dormant or terminated.
