## 0. Thesis — Backend-First Depth vs the Vibecoded App

**The directive in one sentence:** build a manufacturing networking platform whose three hardest numbers — **the price, the match, and the capacity** — are *computed* from physics, data, and live protocol, none of which an LLM can fabricate at prompt time. A vibecoded marketplace is a directory with a contact form and an LLM that reads self-reported tags; it ships in a weekend and decays into spam in a month, because every load-bearing number in it is *asserted, not computed*. PRISM's defensibility is that those numbers are derived.

**But the red-team verdict, folded in up front and not buried (see §7):** *the engineering moat is more real than most "AI marketplace" pitches, but the business is at structural risk of dying of liquidity starvation before the moat ever matters.* The moat is real on the **supplier-tooling axis** (physics quoting, observed capacity, ERP loop) and weak-to-nonexistent on the **marketplace axis** (network effects, liquidity). Therefore this plan adopts an **inverted go-to-market**: ship the shop tool first, weaponize it as supply-side acquisition, **liquidity-gate** the marketplace launch, and reframe physics from a buyer billboard into a supply flywheel + margin engine. We do NOT benchmark against "can a clone be built in a weekend" — the real adversaries are funded companies with 10-year head starts and owned demand. The honest moat is *"the best instant-quote + capacity + ERP tool for a discrete-parts CNC shop,"* and the marketplace is a liquidity bet layered on top.

## 4. The Moat — Concrete, Engine-Named, Honestly Caveated

A vibecoded marketplace fabricates the price, the match, and the capacity at prompt time. PRISM computes all three. **But two of the four moats below rest on foundations that do not yet exist — flagged in bold.**

**Moat 1 — The price is derived, not interpolated (verified: `InstantQuoteEngine.ts`, WIRED).**
Xometry/Protolabs price by ML interpolation over their own historical jobs — a conversion-rate function that *cannot explain why*, *cannot extrapolate to unseen geometry*, and silently mis-prices anything off its training distribution (hence Xometry's documented ~2x repeat-part swings). PRISM inverts this: it reads the actual design artifact (native STEP/AP242-PMI/IGES/DXF + Claude-Vision OCR) and *computes* the quote first-principles — Kienzle specific-cutting-force → power → physics cycle-time (`SpeedFeedOrchestratorEngine`) → Taylor/Bayesian tool-life amortization → MRR/Merchant/chatter-SLD feasibility → real collision validation. Every quote carries `physics_engines_used[]`, a `cycle_time_source` ladder (`cam_derived > physics_calculated > parametric > historical`), per-DFM-issue `physics_basis`, and CI95 bounds. Requires ISO-grounded constants (`src/physics/constants.ts`: kc1.1 P=1800/M=2100/K=1100/N=700/S=2800/H=3200), not a prompt. **The quote is explainable, defensible, and generalizes to never-before-seen parts** — physics needs no training example. *This moat is real, wired, and largely already shipped — it is the MVP.*

**Moat 2 — The match is physics-true, not tag-true (components real; product net-new).**
Every competitor (and Axhera) matches on *self-reported* shop tags. PRISM's capability engines (`MachineCapabilityIntelligenceEngine`, `ForceCapabilityEngine`, `ProcessCapabilityPredictionEngine`) compute *from spindle torque curves, work envelopes, and Cpk* whether a shop can **actually hold this tolerance in this material** — a provable stamp replacing "trust me." `TOPSISEngine`+`MachineMatcherEngine`+`PartSimilarityEngine` rank by *evidence*, not advertising. **HONEST CAVEAT (red-team P0):** (a) the physics-capability engines today model **JM Die's own 21 machines** via single-tenant `ShopConfigurationEngine` — the **multi-tenant `SupplierCapabilityProfile` node + the verification pipeline that ingests an external shop's machine list and stamps it is NET-NEW, and is the *actual* product** (the physics engines are its inputs, not the thing itself). (b) The **GraphSAGE GNN matcher is vaporware**: `NN-GRAPH-MS0.json` shows `auroc_measured: null`, `deploy_gate: "deferred-no-checkpoint"`, `status: "shipped-research-only"` — **no trained checkpoint exists anywhere** (the plan's earlier "checkpoint lives in main" claim is FALSE; only the architecture/pipeline lives in main, the weights were never produced). A link-prediction GNN needs award/outcome history that only exists *after* the marketplace runs — it is structurally a Phase-3+ item. **Production matching is rules-based TOPSIS over real capability data; the GNN is shadow-scored until `auroc_measured` is finite and beats the TOPSIS baseline (gate: ≥0.75).**

**Moat 3 — The capacity is observed, not asserted (decoder verified; live feed net-new).**
Axhera's headline feature — "real-time capacity" — is a shop manually toggling a flag. PRISM owns the ASME MTC1.4 MTConnect decoder (`MTConnectLiveStatusEngine`, 70 actions, `is_running`/`is_idle`/`is_starved`), feeding a TOC/DBR `CapacityPlanningEngine`. No vibecoder builds a standards-compliant MTConnect stack + scheduling brain in a weekend. **HONEST CAVEAT (red-team P1):** the decoder accepts *already-parsed XML only* (0 polling/ingest code); there is **no `OpenCapacityBridgeEngine`, no continuous 1–10 Hz poller daemon, and no `open_capacity` projection anywhere.** Worse, MTConnect has real **install friction** — small shops run mixed-vintage/air-gapped controllers, many have no MTConnect at all, and IT-security objections to piping spindle data to a third party are common; the differentiator may be **un-deployable at the long tail of shops we most need.** Observed-capacity is therefore a **Phase-3 hardware-integration program**, not a Phase-2 software feature. The honest interim (Phase 2) is **scheduling-derived projected capacity** from the wired `CapacityPlanningEngine`+`SchedulingEngine` — "available based on committed job load," competitive and honest, with a graceful-degradation ladder (live telemetry → shop-app-reported → schedule-derived → self-reported = Axhera parity).

**Moat 4 — The integrated ERP loop closes the economics competitors can't see (verified; the durable supply moat).**
`QuoteToShipOrchestratorEngine` runs the atomic quote→order→WO→traveler→invoice→GL spine with hard double-entry invariants (`hotel-financial-invariant-guard.mjs` refuses imbalanced writes) and a 7-vendor ERP round-trip (JobBOSS/Epicor/ProShop/Global Shop/SAP/Oracle/Generic). A won RFQ flows into the *supplier's real ERP* and reconciles **quoted-vs-actual cost per category** (`ERPCostFeedbackEngine`: material/labor/machine-hr/overhead/freight) — then `quote_outcome_feed` re-fits the actual Kienzle kc1.1 / Taylor C,n constants from reality. **The quote gets more accurate over time, with explainable physics deltas — not a frozen black box.** Xometry's Workcenter and Fictiv's portal are captive to *their own* job stream; PRISM owns the shop's whole business, so the shop has a reason to live in the tool daily even when not bidding. **This — plus observed capacity once deployed — is where the durable, compounding *supply-data* moat lives, and it is the one a competitor cannot press-release away.**

**The honest competitive truth:** Xometry genuinely leads on **owned top-of-funnel** (Thomasnet) and **supplier fintech** — neither buyable overnight; we partner/wrap Stripe on the trust-balanced GL core rather than rebuild it. The UI is the cheap half a competitor clones in a weekend; the physics, the data model, and the ERP loop are the half that takes years of manufacturing-domain depth — that is the entire moat.

## 7. Risks + Mitigations (red-team — honest, not buried)

> **Top-line verdict:** a *correctly re-scoped* MVP is buildable mostly in-galaxy; the MVP *as originally framed* is not, because it conflated "engine on disk" with "capability shipped" and rested two headline moats on assets that do not exist. The five risks below are folded into the roadmap above — they are not appendix material.

### Risk 1 — Cold-start liquidity death (CRITICAL, the riskiest assumption — see §8)
A seeded supplier directory ≠ responsive bids. A scraped/seeded shop record that has never heard of you produces bid latency of days-to-never — worse than calling shops yourself. Buyers churn after one dead RFQ.
**Mitigation (baked into Phase 0/1 sequencing):** **invert the roadmap — supply-tool first, marketplace second.** Ship Phase 0 as a *standalone shop product* (physics quoting + ERP loop) with zero buyer dependency; instrument daily-active shops. Flip RFQ-broadcast ON only where **N≥20 daily-active capacity-broadcasting shops** exist; run a concierge phase (hand-broker the first ~100 RFQs) to manufacture liquidity. **Never broadcast into dead supply.**

### Risk 2 — Wrong buyer, wrong value prop (CRITICAL)
Physics/transparency is an *engineer's* nice-to-have sold to a *procurement manager* who buys on speed + reliability + "won't get me fired." A Kienzle-derivation panel is noise — possibly negative signal — to the budget-holder.
**Mitigation:** physics quoting is the **supply-side flywheel + margin engine** (we quote more accurately → better unit economics), NOT the demand-side billboard. Market to shops as *"never underquote again"*; market to buyers as the *outcome* (*"same shop every reorder, honest guaranteed price, verified capability, on-time"*) — bury the physics in the engine room. Pick ONE beachhead persona: **repeat-order, tolerance-sensitive, low-mix buyers** (the exact segment Xometry's price-swing + shop-shuffle hurts most). Validate with 10 design-partner buyers *before* Phase 1.

### Risk 3 — Incumbent fast-neutralization + Axhera already ahead (HIGH)
Our two sharpest *buyer-facing* edges are cheaply copied: Xometry kills the 2x-swing attack with a price-match policy (a press release, not an engine); "direct/named shop" is already Axhera's positioning.
**Mitigation:** anchor the durable moat where a press release can't reach — **the ERP quote-vs-actual loop + observed capacity**, both requiring *physical integration into the shop*. The defensible asset is *"we have live capacity + real cost data on N engaged shops"* (compounding supply-data), not *"we explain quotes"* (a UI cloned in a sprint). Treat **Axhera, not Xometry, as the primary intel target** — we're fast-following them; beachhead where their supply is thin.

### Risk 4 — MTConnect install friction kills the headline differentiator (HIGH)
Observed-capacity depends on shops installing MTConnect adapters and exposing live machine telemetry to a third party. Mixed-vintage/air-gapped controllers, IT-security objections, no-MTConnect-at-all at the long tail — the differentiator may be un-deployable where we most need it.
**Mitigation (baked into Phase 2/3 split):** survey real target-shop MTConnect penetration *now* — don't assume it. Ship the **graceful-degradation ladder**: live telemetry (premium tier) → shop-app-reported → schedule-derived (Phase-2 default) → self-reported (Axhera parity, floor). Offer a turnkey adapter/edge-box for high-value shops. **Never let the moat be a feature 70% of supply can't turn on** — Phase 2 ships schedule-projected capacity; live telemetry is the gated premium upgrade.

### Risk 5 — Building the un-cloneable layer before liquidity exists to feed it (HIGH / capital risk)
The GNN matcher and observed-capacity loop need transaction/hardware volume that only liquidity produces. Building them first burns scarce capital on a flywheel with no fuel — the "vibecoded hype" accusation made concrete: sophisticated engine-building, no paying customers. (Ground truth: `auroc_measured: null`, `deploy_gate: deferred-no-checkpoint` — the matcher has never been trained.)
**Mitigation:** **sequence by liquidity milestones, not engineering elegance.** Rules-based TOPSIS over real capability is sufficient and honest until ≥500 completed transactions exist; a heuristic on real data beats a GNN trained on 12 transactions. Gate Phase 3 (GNN/live-capacity) behind hard thresholds (≥500 quality outcomes; AUROC ≥ baseline; ≥1 machine's telemetry proven to a portal). Spend the saved engineering on demand-gen + supply-onboarding.

### Integration risks (most underestimated)
- **Multi-slot merge is the dominant risk, not bookkeeping.** 10 slots × gap engines × wire-in-main = an integration *project*. The UNWIRED `VendorEngine` (on disk + tested, still 0 dispatcher refs) is the canary for the "90% done, uninvokable" failure mode. **Mitigation:** Phase-0 collapses the fan-out — ONE slot (hotel) wires all Phase-0 actions in a single integration branch; CI gate `npm run build && node scripts/wiring-audit` must show 0 named-action orphans; the round-trip E2E asserts through the *merged main* dispatcher. Stop counting "engine on disk" as progress.
- **The `/api/v1` server handlers are assumed, not verified.** Typed SPA clients exist (`portal.ts`, `machineLive.ts`, `traveler.ts`); the port-3100 bridge route handlers were **not confirmed under `src/`**. **Mitigation:** Phase-0 Unit 0 = `curl` one real `/api/v1` round-trip and assert real JSON *before any UI*; if missing, building the bridge layer IS the Phase-0 foundation (net-new, scoped explicitly).
- **Doc-drift P0:** frontend-app MEMORY claims Next.js 15 at `web/app`; reality is **Vite + React Router SPA at `web/src/pages`, `API_BASE='/api/v1'`, no `web/app`.** Any frontend builder must target the SPA and correct the MEMORY in the same commit.
- **Ollama liveness is a single point of failure** for any offload-dependent step. It is alive now (qwen2.5-coder:7b) but that's host-and-moment-specific. **Mitigation:** every Ollama-routed step needs a Claude/deterministic fallback specified, or it's an SPOF.

## 8. The Single Riskiest Assumption

> **That a seeded supplier directory equals responsive, liquid supply. It does not.**

The entire marketplace thesis is downstream of this one unvalidated belief. A directory of 174 (or 50,000) shop records that have never logged in produces dead RFQs, and buyers churn after the first one. This is why the plan **inverts**: the physics-quoting + ERP tool is a *standalone product a shop wants with zero buyers* — it is the entry drug that accumulates engaged supply + real capability data, and ONLY THEN do we flip on demand against liquid, responsive supply. Keep selling this as "the physics-priced Xometry-killer marketplace" and it most likely dies of liquidity starvation with an impressive engine count and no customers. Ship the honest slice — supply tool first, liquidity-gated marketplace second — and the moat survives contact with the market.
